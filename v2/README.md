# Growth & Impact at Sahaj (Field Guide V2)

V2 sits beside V1 rather than replacing it. V1 (the repo root) is unchanged and still served at `/`; V2 is self-contained in this folder and served at `/v2/`. The existing Pages workflow uploads the whole repo, so no CI change is needed.

Where the work stands, what needs sign-off and what's still open is in [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md).

## Run it

```bash
npm run dev:v2      # live-server with reload, opens /v2/
```

There is no build step. The site fetches `content/*.md` at runtime and renders with native ES modules, so it must be served over HTTP rather than opened as a file.

## The model

**4 scopes × 3 areas**, with each area's expectations grouped: three facets of one or two statements each in Client & Delivery and People & Team, and shared expectations plus ways to contribute in Org & Community.

| Scope | Radius | Replaces (V1) |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | Impact through work directly in your hands (your own stories and features) | Artisan, late Explorer |
| Extended | Improves work beyond your own (others' features, a stream, a teammate) | Catalyst |
| Team | Changes how the whole team operates, performs and grows | Multiplier, Amplifier |
| Wider | Crosses the team boundary: the account as a whole, client org, Sahaj or community | Strategist, Pioneer |

Areas: **Client & Delivery** (facets: technical craft, consulting, delivery), **People & Team** (facets: growing others, feedback and trust, communication and collaboration), **Org & Community** (ways to contribute: culture and organisational health, initiatives and capability, community and external contribution).

The decisions behind it:

- **Area-neutral scope names.** Each scope describes how far impact reaches, and each area's cells describe what that means for it. Concrete nouns like Feature only made sense for Client & Delivery. "Project" was dropped as a rung because on many engagements project and team are the same people. The names are deliberately plain so they describe an expectation, never a person.
- **Extended vs Team** is the boundary to protect: improving work beyond your own, versus improving the system that produces the work.
- **One scope per person, uneven strengths within it.** The reference scope reflects impact sustained across all three areas, sets the expectations in every area,. Strength per area is captured by the Below / Meets / Exceeds rating, not by setting areas at different scopes. Growth happens by taking on some of the next scope's responsibilities, usually as a stretch (per area, adding to current expectations). Consistent next-scope impact in one area rates Exceeds, whether or not it was agreed as a stretch; the reference scope is raised at the team check only once next-scope impact is consistent, and relied on, in all three areas. A stretch is not a precondition for a raise. No fixed duration is prescribed. Someone growing quickly can stretch early, in one area or more; someone at the same scope for a long time, or below what their experience suggests, has a stretch set with their reviewers in all three areas.
- **Evidence is a pattern, not an instance.** Evidence prompts ask for consistency, durability and reliance across the cycle, because one good example shows you can, not that you do.
- **Scopes instead of persona names.** Scopes say what they mean, and they resist becoming identities ("I'm a Catalyst").
- **No scope below Direct.** Every hire's scope is agreed from their experience, with Direct as the first; the first weeks of learning a new stack or domain are expected and not a scope of their own. If graduate hiring returns, add a scope file with `order: 0`.
- **Expectations describe impact, not roles.** Each statement names the outcome and how far it reaches; leading a team, an account or a proposal is a route to it, described under "ways to create impact" and in evidence. Otherwise Team and Wider would quietly require a lead role and contradict the multiple routes.
- **Amplifier and Pioneer became "ways to create impact"** at Team and Wider scope, keeping V1's "not the easier path" argument.
- **Facets that all count, ways to contribute that are chosen.** Client & Delivery merges three V1 capabilities and People & Team two, so each lists its expectations under three facets, and all three count toward one rating, judged across them: exceeding the scope's expectations in two facets can carry a weaker third, but a facet not shown at the scope at all makes the area Below, so strong craft still can't hide missing consulting. How it works sets out how the facets combine. Org & Community works differently: one or two expectations shared by everyone at the scope, then ways to contribute to choose from, so it doesn't become a three-part checklist. The page says which is which wherever a group appears (`GRAMMAR` in `js/views/_parts.js`). Facet statements draw on the V1 persona expectations for the matching capability, one or two per facet so an area doesn't grow into a checklist.
- **The guide doesn't discuss pay.** It describes scope and expectations only; how scope relates to compensation is for whoever owns pay to communicate separately.
- **Org & Community is not optional.** Your choice of how, not whether. It happens in working time. No contribution beyond delivery means the area is below expectations; an opportunity gap must be raised during the cycle, with the team's seniors or, if account-wide, the account lead. If it was raised and nothing changed, the person is rated on what was reasonably available and the constraint is recorded in the doc. Org & Community counts toward a raise like any area.
- **Expectations are made visible in a short doc each person drives.** The doc's only job is to make expectations visible, so the self-assessment conversation starts from what was agreed rather than a debate about what was expected. It is one plain template for every scope (`doc-templates/expectations.docx`, generated by `scripts/build_doc_templates.js`, run with `npm run templates:v2`), deliberately minimal rather than a form: name, cycle and scope, "My expectations" (a line per area for anything specific to the person's work or team, how they want to contribute to Org & Community, and where they want to grow), and a short self-assessment ending in where they grew and what next. It is growth-focused, not a record for checking up on people: no sign-offs, dates or approvals. It fits on one page as a single light table: a row per area plus a growth row, with My expectations (filled in at the start) beside Self-assessment (at the end), so the reflection sits next to what it looks back on. The self-rating is a small "My view" line at the foot of each reflection, and a line under the title says it is not a running log. The expectations themselves stay in the portal, which the doc links to, so there is no second copy to drift. People keep feedback notes wherever they like; the doc doesn't collect them. The team's seniors review it and confirm ratings at the team check; a senior is reviewed by their account lead or seniors from other teams, and an account lead by seniors or leads from other accounts in the office. Anyone on the team can review and ask for changes, and the account lead decides when disagreement remains (a founder or the region's managing director, for an account lead's own doc).

## Design

An editorial field guide with practical tools, built on a restrained system: text on warm off-white paper (warm greys throughout, cards and the matrix on white so they lift off the page), IBM Plex Sans throughout, colour only where it means something. Interaction (links, buttons, selected states) is graphite ink, near-black in light mode and near-white in dark, so the three area colours (teal, blue, amber) are the only colour on the page; they mark areas on labels and a 56px bar above each area, never bullets. On scope pages whitespace and that bar separate the three areas, and each area has two levels: its heading and core line on the page, and its detail (facets, ways to contribute and How to tell you're meeting it) on a white reading surface with no border. The In practice panel stays on the neutral tint, because it is an illustration rather than expectations. On Compare and the area page, where the page is laid out by area, each area's opening block (and the area page's header) sits on a faint tint of the area's colour (`--area-soft`, 6% in light mode and 10% in dark), so the three areas stand out when scanning; the tint stays on the opening block, never the long lists below it. Interaction stays ink rather than a fourth hue, so no colour reads as a fourth area. Motion is used only to confirm a choice, never as decoration: the Concise | Detailed switch slides its ink pill in about 0.2s, switched off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Reading pages have no motion. No shadows, one radius for panels (8px) and one for controls (6px), no pills. Notices are neutral grey panels; semantic colours appear only in form validation. Pages come in three styles. Expectations is the landing page (the empty address and old `#home` links open it): the guide's lede and the four Start here task links above the grid, each row's heading carrying the scope's mindset and reach so the grid also explains the scopes, and How growth happens below it. How it works is the most visual reading page, with one situation stepped through all four scopes by hand, right after the scopes are introduced. Scope pages lead with each area's core line and labelled facets, since that is what people open them for, then that situation at their own scope ("In practice", on a neutral panel and marked as an illustration), with evidence, self-checks, examples and ways to create impact in `<details>` that open for printing. Working pages (Self-assessment, Reviewing and feedback) are worksheets: numbered steps, prompt cards, example panels and a scope selector that links the expectations being written against. Facet and route labels are small capitals in the area's colour, which keeps colour on labels rather than panels. Each area also has a stroke icon (`areaIcon` in `js/diagram.js`) beside its name, so it is recognisable by shape as well as colour. On reading pages, each section starts on a hairline with a short ink bar, the area motif in ink; a paragraph marked `<!-- callout -->` is a rule readers must not miss and gets a ruled panel; one section per page marked `<!-- band -->` sits on a tinted band where the register changes; and on worksheets `<!-- scale -->` draws a list as a scale (the ratings) and `<!-- list -->` keeps a list ordinary, so cards stay for what a reader fills in. Visuals are used only where the idea is itself a picture, all in `js/diagram.js` or CSS: the reach diagram (nested outline circles, so it reads as reach rather than rank), the numbered growth strip and the cycle strip on How it works, and the ring diagram, used once there to show a stretch. Expectations has two views, all scopes and compare two, switched with tabs. All scopes opens Detailed, each area's core line with its facets below, and a Concise | Detailed switch above the table keeps only the core lines; the choice is remembered in the browser. On a phone, compare folds the "from" scope's facet lists behind a toggle. The top nav has five items, Reviewing among them. On-page section links update the address, so a copied URL reopens that section. Once someone picks a scope to explore, from the header's Explore a scope menu, it stays visible: "Exploring <scope>" leads the top nav (first in the menu on phones) as a dropdown that changes it from any page (`js/scope-menu.js`): a scope page opens the chosen scope, Compare compares it with the next one up, and other pages re-render in place, and the scope's own page, its matrix row and its row in each area's How it grows carry an Exploring tag. It says exploring, never "your scope", because only the agreed doc sets that. Terms live in `content/glossary.md` (the Glossary page, linked from the footer and Questions); each entry's `<!-- matches: ... -->` lists the phrases that count as a mention, and `js/terms.js` links the first mention of each term on a reading page (How it works, area pages, the worksheets and Questions; never scope pages, Compare or the Expectations grid) with a dotted underline and a one-sentence hover preview. The theme follows the system unless someone uses the toggle. The favicon (`favicon.svg`) is the header's reach mark, drawn heavier so it holds at tab size and switching to light ink in dark mode; V1 keeps its compass, so the two tabs are easy to tell apart.

## Content conventions

Scope files (`content/scopes/*.md`) have frontmatter (`id`, `name`, `order`, `reach`, `how`, `lasts`, `reaches`, `mindset`, `question`, `v1`; `reach`, `how` and `lasts` are the nature of impact shown under each scope's mindset and side by side on Compare; `reaches` is the short `·`-separated list under each scope's mindset in the Expectations grid's row headings). In `reach`, `how`, `lasts`, the What changes sentences and each expectation statement, bold (`**…**`) marks the one short phrase that carries the scope: who or what the impact reaches, or how much is owned. Bold the equivalent phrase at every scope, so on Compare the bold phrases line up and the step between scopes shows at a glance; keep it to one phrase a line, and never bold core statements, which are already bold) and these sections:

```markdown
## Summary
## What changes            (from the previous scope)
## In practice             (the shared situation, then "- **What you did**:", "- **What changed**:", "- **Why it's <Scope>**:")
## Client & Delivery       (one per area, matching the area's name)
> The one-line core statement.
### What changes           (one sentence on this area's step from the previous scope; not on Direct)
### Expectations           (#### per facet, one or two statements each; Org & Community: ungrouped shared lines)
### Evidence
### Self-check
### Not yet
### Ways to contribute     (Org & Community only: #### per way to contribute, this scope's examples)
## Ways to create impact   (optional; intro paragraph, then ### per way)
## Examples                (### Meets expectations, ### Below expectations: one paragraph each)
```

Area files (`content/areas/*.md`) have `id`, `name`, `key` (`cd`, `pt`, `oc`), `order` and `question`, then `## Summary`, `## Facets` (or `## Ways to contribute` for Org & Community, with a short intro, plus `## Examples at each scope`, which shows every scope's examples side by side) with `###` per facet or way to contribute, and any further `##` sections, which render as prose.

Renaming a scope or area heading changes what the parser matches, and changing a scope or area `id` breaks links such as `#scope/team/client-delivery`. Treat both as breaking changes.

Pages: `home.md` holds the landing page's words: its lede and two short `##` sections (Start here, How growth happens) are looked up by heading and shown on Expectations, Start here as a list of `- [Task](#route): what it involves` lines, so renaming one needs the matching change in `js/views/matrix.js`. Its `version` is the guide version shown in the footer. The Scopes in practice stepper on How it works takes its intro from that page's `## Scopes in practice` section and each step from a scope's In practice section, with the situation from Direct's, so keep the situation sentence the same in all four scope files (`js/views/how.js`). `how-it-works.md` holds the mechanics, `teammates.md` the guidance for reviewers and `self-assessment.md` the guide to writing a self-assessment; all three render as reading pages (`##` sections), and the last two as worksheets, where a run of `1. **Step**: text` becomes numbered steps, `- **Title**: text` becomes cards and a blockquote becomes an example panel (see `js/views/prose.js`). How it works sections are linkable as `#how-it-works/<slug>`. In How it works, the Scopes, Growing into the next scope and The cycle sections are drawn as visuals from their `- **Title**: text` lists (see `js/views/how.js`), so keep that format and those headings. `faq.md` groups questions: `##` is a group, `###` a question, and each question is linkable as `#faq/<slug>`. Scope sections are linkable as `#scope/<scope>/<area>`.
