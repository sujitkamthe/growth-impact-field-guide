# Growth & Impact at Sahaj (Field Guide V2)

V2 is the guide. It is self-contained in this folder and served at `/v2/`, and the site's root redirects here. The original Field Guide (V1) is kept for reference in `v1/` and served at `/v1/`; the root redirect sends V1-style links (`#personas`, `#persona-…`) there. The Pages workflow uploads the whole repo and builds V1 inside `v1/`.

Where the work stands, what needs sign-off and what's still open is in [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md).

## Run it

```bash
npm run dev:v2      # live-server with reload, opens /v2/
```

There is no build step. The site fetches `content/*.md` at runtime and renders with native ES modules, so it must be served over HTTP rather than opened as a file.

## The model

**4 scopes × 3 areas**, with each area's expectations grouped: three facets of one or two statements each in Client & Delivery and People & Team, and shared expectations plus ways to contribute in Org & Community.

| Scope | Radius | Replaces (V1) |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | Impact through work directly in your hands (your own stories and features) | Artisan, late Explorer |
| Extended | Improves work beyond your own (others' features, a stream, a teammate) | Catalyst |
| Team | Changes how the whole team operates, performs and grows | Multiplier, Amplifier |
| Wider | Crosses the team boundary: the account as a whole, client org, Sahaj or community | Strategist, Pioneer |

Areas: **Client & Delivery** (facets: technical craft, consulting, delivery ownership), **People & Team** (facets: growing others, feedback and trust, communication and collaboration), **Org & Community** (ways to contribute: culture and organisational health, initiatives and capability, community and external contribution).

The decisions behind it:

- **Area-neutral scope names.** Each scope describes how far impact reaches, and each area's cells describe what that means for it. Concrete nouns like Feature only made sense for Client & Delivery. "Project" was dropped as a rung because on many engagements project and team are the same people. The names are deliberately plain so they describe an expectation, never a person.
- **Extended vs Team** is the boundary to protect: improving work beyond your own, versus improving the system that produces the work.
- **One scope per person, uneven strengths within it; scope grows, it isn't raised.** Your scope reflects how far your contribution reaches, sustained across all three areas, and sets the expectations in every area (judged against an agreed focus where there is one). The guide calls it simply your scope; an earlier draft's "reference scope" was one term too many. Strength per area is captured by the Below / Meets / Exceeds rating, not by setting areas at different scopes. Growth includes deepening expertise at the current scope, not only reaching the next one. Reaching further happens by taking on some of the next scope's responsibilities, usually as a stretch, or a greater contribution through a strength. Consistent next-scope impact in one area rates Exceeds; the scope grows at the team check only once the person's own contribution consistently reaches the next scope, and is relied on, in all three areas. "Raise" was dropped as a ladder word that also reads as a pay raise; scopes are nested circles of reach, so a scope grows. A stretch is not a precondition. No fixed duration is prescribed. Someone at the same scope for a long time, or below what their experience suggests, agrees a development goal with their team, which can deepen their contribution or prepare them for the next scope; there is no compulsory stretch in all three areas, which also couldn't work at Wider.
- **Evidence is a pattern, not an instance.** Evidence prompts ask for consistency, durability and reliance across the cycle, because one good example shows you can, not that you do.
- **Scopes instead of persona names.** Scopes say what they mean, and they resist becoming identities ("I'm a Catalyst").
- **No scope below Direct.** Every hire's scope is agreed from their experience, with Direct as the first; the first weeks of learning a new stack or domain are expected and not a scope of their own. If graduate hiring returns, add a scope file with `order: 0`.
- **Expectations describe impact, not roles.** Each statement names the outcome and how far it reaches; leading a team, an account or a proposal is a route to it, described under "ways to create impact" and in evidence. Otherwise Team and Wider would quietly require a lead role and contradict the multiple routes.
- **Amplifier and Pioneer became "ways to create impact"** at Team and Wider scope, keeping V1's "not the easier path" argument.
- **One home per rule, and a path for the three tasks.** Each rule is written once and other pages link to it: the model, review and escalation, and growing your scope in How it works; the rating scale (with "weaker" and "missing" defined: weaker is shown at your scope but not yet consistently, missing is shown only at the previous scope or not at all), the first cycle and a cycle without notes in Self-assessment; feedback technique in Feedback; the Org & Community deferral in its area page; agreed focus in Agreed focus. The nav follows the tasks (How it works · All scopes · Agree expectations · Feedback · Self-assessment · Questions); Principles and Reviewing a teammate sit in the footer and on Start. Agree your expectations (`content/agree.md`) is the worksheet for writing the doc, with a finished doc for the same Team-scope person whose self-assessment is the worked example. Scope pages' Concise shows each expectation's sentence after its handle, so a handle never reads as a bare task; the grid and the area page's How it grows stay handles only.
- **Facets that all count, ways to contribute that are chosen.** Client & Delivery merges three V1 capabilities and People & Team two, so each lists its expectations under three facets, and all three count toward one rating, judged across them: one facet can be weaker when the others clearly go beyond the scope, but a facet shown only at the previous scope or not at all makes the area Below, so strong craft still can't hide missing consulting. The rule is stated once, in Self-assessment's rating scale, and other pages link to it rather than restating it. Org & Community works differently: one or two expectations shared by everyone at the scope, then ways to contribute to choose from, so it doesn't become a three-part checklist. The page says which is which wherever a group appears (`GRAMMAR` in `js/views/_parts.js`). Facet statements draw on the V1 persona expectations for the matching capability, one or two per facet so an area doesn't grow into a checklist.
- **All areas for everyone; a shape earned through depth.** All three areas are part of what's expected of everyone, and people choose their activities within each (a talk instead of writing needs no agreement). At Extended scope and above, an *agreed focus* lets someone take less of particular responsibilities (leading architecture across the engagement, discovery or client negotiations, coordinating the team's delivery, formal mentoring), which a named teammate who has room for them takes on, in exchange for a greater contribution through depth already shown, measured from the next scope's relevant expectations (agreed directly at Wider). Standards stay with everyone: engineering standards in your own work, understanding the client's problem, realistic personal commitments, working respectfully and giving feedback, and an Org & Community contribution. It is described by the contribution, not by weaknesses, so it can't become a way to opt out; someone still building expertise can agree a one-cycle trial. Direct is excluded because it is where people learn every part of the work. Each area, and the scope, is judged against the agreed focus, so the arrangement doesn't block growth. The greater contribution is required (a substantial shortfall isn't Meets) and can earn Exceeds; Exceeds can also come through exceptional impact in one facet when the rest of the area is met. It lives on one page, Agreed focus (`content/specialisation.md`, `#specialisation`), with its rules, rating and examples, so it costs nothing to the people who don't need one; How it works mentions it in one sentence.
- **Delivery means delivery ownership.** The facet was renamed from "Delivery" because people read the bare word as finishing stories on time. It covers commitments and outcomes: agreeing what can realistically be delivered, managing the client's expectations, and holding a position with reasons and an alternative when a date, scope or pace isn't safe. Pushing back on what to build is consulting; pushing back on commitments is delivery ownership, so a hard conversation has one home.
- **Feedback covers all of someone's impact, and every team check looks ahead.** Feedback reaches all three areas, recognises and asks for more as well as correcting, and reaches the people already doing well. Because cycles are quarterly and each ends in a rated team check, there is no separate mid-cycle check: each team check also looks ahead at missing evidence, especially in an area or facet nobody has commented on, and agrees what changes next cycle, so no quarter is saved up (`content/feedback.md`).
- **The guide doesn't discuss pay.** It describes scope and expectations only; how scope relates to compensation is for whoever owns pay to communicate separately.
- **Org & Community is not optional.** Your choice of how, not whether. It happens in working time. No contribution beyond delivery means the area is below expectations; an opportunity gap is agreed with the team's seniors or, if account-wide, the account lead, recorded at the start of the cycle when it is known then (such as a new account being set up) or raised as soon as it arises, with what is still possible, who makes the time and a review date. The person is rated on what was reasonably available and the constraint is recorded in the doc; a deferral that covered the whole cycle is recorded as not rated, with the reason, and doesn't count toward the scope growing. Org & Community counts toward scope growth like any area.
- **Expectations are made visible in a short doc each person drives.** The doc's only purpose is to make expectations visible, so the self-assessment conversation starts from what was agreed rather than a debate about what was expected. It is one plain template for every scope (`doc-templates/expectations.docx`, generated by `scripts/build_doc_templates.js`, run with `npm run templates:v2`), deliberately minimal rather than a form: name, cycle and scope, "My expectations" (a line per area for anything specific to the person's work or team, how they want to contribute to Org & Community, and where they want to grow), and a short self-assessment ending in where they grew and what next. It is growth-focused, not a record for checking up on people: no sign-offs, dates or approvals. It fits on one page as a single light table: a row per area plus a growth row, with My expectations (filled in at the start) beside Self-assessment (at the end), so the reflection sits next to what it looks back on. The self-rating is a small "My view" line at the foot of each reflection, and a line under the title says it is not a running log. The expectations themselves stay in the portal, which the doc links to, so there is no second copy to drift. People keep feedback notes wherever they like; the doc doesn't collect them. Anyone on the team can review it and ask for changes, and teammates confirm ratings at the team check. The guide deliberately names no reviewer chain by seniority, so review goes in every direction. When disagreement remains, the team's seniors make a judgement call (leaving out the person whose doc it is), then the account lead decides (a founder or the region's managing director, for an account lead's own doc).

## Design

An editorial field guide with practical tools, built on a restrained system: text on warm off-white paper (warm greys throughout, cards and the matrix on white so they lift off the page), IBM Plex Sans throughout, colour only where it means something. Interaction (links, buttons, selected states) is graphite ink, near-black in light mode and near-white in dark, so the three area colours (teal, blue, amber) are the only colour on the page; they mark areas on labels and a 56px bar above each area, never bullets. On scope pages whitespace and that bar separate the three areas, and each area has two levels: its heading and core line on the page, and its detail (facets, ways to contribute and How to tell you're meeting it) on a white reading surface with no border. The In practice panel stays on the neutral tint, because it is an illustration rather than expectations. On Compare and the area page, where the page is laid out by area, each area's opening block (and the area page's header) sits on a faint tint of the area's colour (`--area-soft`, 6% in light mode and 10% in dark), so the three areas stand out when scanning; the tint stays on the opening block, never the long lists below it. Interaction stays ink rather than a fourth hue, so no colour reads as a fourth area. Motion is used only to confirm a choice, never as decoration: the Concise | Detailed switch slides its ink pill in about 0.2s, switched off under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Reading pages have no motion. No shadows, one radius for panels (8px) and one for controls (6px), no pills. Notices are neutral grey panels; semantic colours appear only in form validation. Pages come in three styles. Start is the landing page (the empty address and `#home`, including old `#home/<section>` links): how the guide works beside the reach diagram, the four scopes as cards to find your own, and the task links, so a new joiner meets scopes and areas before any expectation. Expectations follows it: each row's heading carrying the scope's mindset and reach so the grid also explains the scopes, and How growth happens below it. How it works is the most visual reading page, with one situation stepped through all four scopes by hand, right after the scopes are introduced. Scope pages lead with each area's core line and labelled facets, since that is what people open them for, then that situation at their own scope ("In practice", on a neutral panel and marked as an illustration), with evidence, self-checks, examples and ways to create impact in `<details>` that open for printing. Working pages (Agree your expectations, Feedback, Self-assessment, Reviewing a teammate) are worksheets, each shaped by its task rather than one bullet layout for all: Agree shows the expectations doc itself as a doc sheet (a table with an area-coloured bar per row: what to write beside a line from a finished doc) and the teammates' checks as an unordered checklist; Self-assessment shows the ratings as one horizontal scale and its worked example as the same doc with the self-assessment column filled in, so the two pages read as the start and end of one doc; Feedback is a set of topics rather than numbered steps (`flow: topics` in its frontmatter), led by one annotated note (`<!-- note -->`: what you saw, what it changed, what you'd suggest), with area cards in their colours and each example beside the idea it shows. Worksheets run wider than reading pages (940px) so two to four short cards sit side by side, while running text keeps the reading measure; steps can sit in a row (`<!-- row -->`). The map of a page's parts appears only when there are more than three, because with three the parts themselves are already in view. A scope selector links the expectations being written against. Facet and route labels are small capitals in the area's colour, which keeps colour on labels rather than panels. Each area also has a stroke icon (`areaIcon` in `js/diagram.js`) beside its name, so it is recognisable by shape as well as colour. On reading pages, each section starts on a hairline with a short ink bar, the area motif in ink; a paragraph marked `<!-- callout -->` is a rule readers must not miss and gets a ruled panel; one section per page marked `<!-- band -->` sits on a tinted band where the register changes; and on worksheets `<!-- scale -->` draws a list as a scale (the ratings) and `<!-- list -->` keeps a list ordinary, so cards stay for what a reader fills in. Visuals are used only where the idea is itself a picture, all in `js/diagram.js` or CSS: the reach diagram (nested outline circles, so it reads as reach rather than rank), the numbered growth strip and the cycle strip on How it works, and the ring diagram, used once there to show a stretch. Each principle has a small line drawing in the same ink style, showing its idea rather than an icon for it, so the page can be taken in at a glance. Expectations has two views, all scopes and compare two, switched with tabs. Once someone explores a scope, All scopes shows only that scope's row, with a link to compare it with the next scope and a Show all levels button beside the switch (remembered in the browser); a first visit, with no scope chosen, sees every row. All scopes opens Concise, each area's core line with its facets below listing only the handles, so the grid reads like a one-page sheet; the Concise | Detailed switch above the table puts each handle over the bullets that unpack it, and the choice is remembered in the browser. Scope pages open Concise too, with the three areas side by side on wide screens and a Concise | Detailed switch in their sticky bar (remembered separately); the area page's How it grows lists only the handles, and Compare shows the Concise form, so the step between scopes reads line by line. On a phone, compare folds the "from" scope's facet lists behind a toggle. The top nav has six items, Reviewing among them; the brand links to Start. Pages have no "on this page" link lists: sections are short enough to take in at a glance, and each is still linkable by address, which opens it if it is folded. Once someone picks a scope to explore, from the header's Explore a scope menu, it stays visible: "Exploring <scope>" leads the top nav (first in the menu on phones) as a dropdown that changes it from any page (`js/scope-menu.js`): a scope page opens the chosen scope, Compare compares it with the next one up, and other pages re-render in place, and the scope's own page, its matrix row and its row in each area's How it grows carry an Exploring tag. It says exploring, never "your scope", because only the agreed doc sets that. Terms live in `content/glossary.md` (the Glossary page, linked from the footer and Questions); each entry's `<!-- matches: ... -->` lists the phrases that count as a mention, and `js/terms.js` links the first mention of each term on a reading page (How it works, area pages, the worksheets, Principles and Questions; never scope pages, Compare or the Expectations grid) with a dotted underline and a one-sentence hover preview, but only inside text the reader has opened (Read more, a principle, an answer), so a page's first view isn't dotted with links. The theme follows the system unless someone uses the toggle. The favicon (`favicon.svg`) is the header's reach mark, drawn heavier so it holds at tab size and switching to light ink in dark mode; V1 keeps its compass, so the two tabs are easy to tell apart.

## Content conventions

Scope files (`content/scopes/*.md`) have frontmatter (`id`, `name`, `order`, `reach`, `how`, `lasts`, `reaches`, `mindset`, `question`, `v1`; `reach`, `how` and `lasts` are the nature of impact shown under each scope's mindset and side by side on Compare; `reaches` is the short `·`-separated list under each scope's mindset in the Expectations grid's row headings). In `reach`, `how`, `lasts`, the What changes sentences and each expectation statement, bold (`**…**`) marks the one short phrase that carries the scope: who or what the impact reaches, or how much is owned. Bold the equivalent phrase at every scope, so on Compare the bold phrases line up and the step between scopes shows at a glance; keep it to one phrase a line, and never bold core statements, which are already bold. Each expectation opens with a handle, a plain verb phrase of five words or fewer that names what someone does, in the spirit of the UK self-assessment sheet; a line without a handle or bullets still renders as its sentence) and these sections:

```markdown
## Summary
## What changes            (from the previous scope)
## In practice             (the shared situation, then "- **What you did**:", "- **What changed**:", "- **Why it's <Scope>**:")
## Client & Delivery       (one per area, matching the area's name)
> The one-line core statement.
### What changes           (one sentence on this area's step from the previous scope; not on Direct)
### Expectations           (#### per facet, one or two expectations each; Org & Community: ungrouped shared lines)
- **Handle**: One sentence  (a short verb phrase, then the sentence Concise shows)
  - Detail bullet           (two or three indented bullets that unpack it, shown in Detailed; bold one key phrase in each. Start with the verb, not "I": the core line and sentence already speak in the first person, so a run of "I …" bullets only delays the action. Keep "my" or "I" inside a bullet where it says who owns something ("the defects I introduce", "my stream"), and keep an outcome line's own subject ("The team keeps using it…"))
### Evidence
### Self-check
### Not yet
### Ways to contribute     (Org & Community only: #### per way to contribute, this scope's examples)
## Ways to create impact   (optional; intro paragraph, then ### per way)
## Examples                (### Meets expectations, ### Below expectations: one paragraph each)
```

Area files (`content/areas/*.md`) have `id`, `name`, `key` (`cd`, `pt`, `oc`), `order` and `question`, then `## Summary`, `## Facets` (or `## Ways to contribute` for Org & Community, with a short intro, plus `## Examples at each scope`, which shows every scope's examples side by side) with `###` per facet or way to contribute, and any further `##` sections, which render as prose.

Renaming a scope or area heading changes what the parser matches, and changing a scope or area `id` breaks links such as `#scope/team/client-delivery`. Treat both as breaking changes.

Pages: `home.md` holds the landing page's words: its headline, lede and `##` sections are looked up by heading. How the guide works, Find your scope and Where to go next (a list of `- [Task](#route): what it involves` lines) are shown on Start (`js/views/start.js`), and How growth happens under the Expectations grid (`js/views/matrix.js`), so renaming one needs the matching change in that view. Start's reach diagram reads How it works' Scopes list, so the two always match. Its `version` is the guide version shown in the footer. The Scopes in practice stepper on How it works takes its intro from that page's `## Scopes in practice` section and each step from a scope's In practice section, with the situation from Direct's, so keep the situation sentence the same in all four scope files (`js/views/how.js`). `principles.md` holds the Principles page, read before the scopes and areas: its `## Our principles` list of `- **Name**: *Short line.* Full text` lines is shown as tiles, each with a line drawing of its idea, its name and the short line, and the full text opens from the tile (`js/views/principles.js`). Drawings are matched in list order, so keep that format, keep the short line to about ten words, and draw one if you add a principle. `how-it-works.md` holds the mechanics, `teammates.md` the guidance for teammates reading a doc and taking part in a team check, `agree.md` the worksheet for writing your expectations, `feedback.md` giving and asking for feedback and how each team check looks ahead, and `self-assessment.md` the guide to writing a self-assessment; all of them render as reading pages (`##` sections), kept to what can be taken in at a glance: a section that opens with a short line in italics (`*One sentence.*`, after any `<!-- band -->`) shows only that line and folds the rest behind Read more, and `<!-- more -->` keeps what comes before it in view and folds the rest, so steps or cards can stay visible while their explanation folds (area page notes follow the same rules; see `glance` in `js/views/prose.js`), and a page whose sections carry `<!-- group: Title | one line | icon -->` markers reads as a journey: a map of numbered parts at the top, each part on a numbered rail holding the sections up to the next marker, and `<!-- tips: Title -->` gathering optional sections as cards at the end, so a reader can see where to start and what to skip (How it works by part of the model, Self-assessment as three steps, Reviewing by when in the cycle; see `journey` in `js/views/prose.js`; icon names are those in `js/icons.js`, the shared line icons used on links that send the reader somewhere and on folded rows), and the last two as worksheets, where a run of `1. **Step**: text` becomes numbered steps, `- **Title**: text` becomes cards and a blockquote becomes an example panel (see `js/views/prose.js`). How it works sections are linkable as `#how-it-works/<slug>`. In How it works, the Scopes, Growing into the next scope and The cycle sections are drawn as visuals from their `- **Title**: text` lists (see `js/views/how.js`), so keep that format and those headings. `faq.md` groups questions: `##` is a group, `###` a question, and each question is linkable as `#faq/<slug>`. Scope sections are linkable as `#scope/<scope>/<area>`.
