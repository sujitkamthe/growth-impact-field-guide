# Growth & Impact at Sahaj (Field Guide V2)

V2 sits beside V1 rather than replacing it. V1 (the repo root) is unchanged and still served at `/`; V2 is self-contained in this folder and served at `/v2/`. The existing Pages workflow uploads the whole repo, so no CI change is needed.

Where the work stands, what needs sign-off and what's still open is in [HANDOFF.md](HANDOFF.md).

## Run it

```bash
npm run dev:v2      # live-server with reload, opens /v2/
```

There is no build step. The site fetches `content/*.md` at runtime and renders with native ES modules, so it must be served over HTTP rather than opened as a file.

## The model

**4 scopes × 3 areas**, with at most four expectations per cell.

| Scope | Radius | Replaces (V1) |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | Impact through work directly in your hands (your own stories and features) | Artisan, late Explorer |
| Extended | Improves work beyond your own (others' features, a stream, a teammate) | Catalyst |
| Team | Changes how the whole team operates, performs and grows | Multiplier, Amplifier |
| Wider | Crosses the team boundary: the account as a whole, client org, Sahaj or community | Strategist, Pioneer |

Areas: **Client & Delivery** (craft, consulting, delivery), **People & Team** (mentoring, feedback, communication), **Org & Community** (culture, initiatives, community).

The decisions behind it:

- **Area-neutral scope names.** Each scope describes how far impact reaches, and each area's cells describe what that means for it. Concrete nouns like Feature only made sense for Client & Delivery. "Project" was dropped as a rung because on many engagements project and team are the same people. The names are deliberately plain so they describe an expectation, never a person.
- **Extended vs Team** is the boundary to protect: improving work beyond your own, versus improving the system that produces the work.
- **One scope per person, uneven strengths within it.** The reference scope reflects impact sustained across all three areas, sets the expectations in every area,. Strength per area is captured by the Below / Meets / Exceeds rating, not by setting areas at different scopes. Growth happens by taking on some of the next scope's responsibilities as a stretch (per area, adding to current expectations) until they are sustained.
- **Evidence is a pattern, not an instance.** Evidence prompts ask for consistency, durability and reliance across the cycle, because one good example shows you can, not that you do.
- **Scopes instead of persona names.** Scopes say what they mean, and they resist becoming identities ("I'm a Catalyst").
- **No scope below Direct.** Every hire's scope is agreed from their experience, with Direct as the first; the first weeks of learning a new stack or domain are expected and not a scope of their own. If graduate hiring returns, add a scope file with `order: 0`.
- **Expectations describe impact, not roles.** Each statement names the outcome and how far it reaches; leading a team, an account or a proposal is a route to it, described under "ways to create impact" and in evidence. Otherwise Team and Wider would quietly require a lead role and contradict the multiple routes.
- **Amplifier and Pioneer became "ways to create impact"** at Team and Wider scope, keeping V1's "not the easier path" argument.
- **Three areas, facets kept visible.** Client & Delivery merges three V1 capabilities; its page says a gap in one facet is still a gap, so strong craft can't average out weak consulting.
- **The guide doesn't discuss pay.** It describes scope and expectations only; how scope relates to compensation is for whoever owns pay to communicate separately.
- **Org & Community is not optional.** Your choice of how, not whether. No contribution beyond delivery means the area is below expectations; an opportunity gap must be raised during the cycle, not at assessment.
- **Expectations are written down, outside the portal, and each person drives their own doc.** The portal explains the model; people record expectations, feedback and self-assessment in one Google Doc per cycle, made from a scope template in `doc-templates/` (Word files generated by `scripts/build_doc_templates.js`, run with `npm run templates:v2`; regenerate after content changes). The person chooses their scope, drafts the doc and shares it; senior teammates usually review it, and seniors are reviewed by seniors on other projects in the account or office. Feedback goes into the same doc through the cycle, so the self-assessment starts from evidence, and the team check can work from the doc. `content/self-assessment.md` (rating definitions, describing a shortfall, common traps) is no longer a page and is kept as source material for that doc template.

## Design

Clean and minimal, to avoid information overload: text on white, IBM Plex Sans throughout, colour only where it means something. Interaction (links, buttons, selected states) is graphite ink, near-black in light mode and near-white in dark, so the three area colours (teal, blue, ochre) are the only colour on the page; they mark areas on labels and 2px rules, never bullets or panels. No shadows, one radius for panels (8px) and one for controls (6px), no pills. Notices are neutral grey panels; semantic colours appear only in form validation. Pages lead with what matters and put the rest one click away: scope pages show each area's core line and expectations, with evidence, self-checks, examples and routes in `<details>` that open for printing. Visuals are used only where the idea is itself a picture, all in `js/diagram.js` or CSS: the reach diagram (nested outline circles, so it reads as reach rather than rank), the numbered growth strip and the cycle strip on How it works, and the ring diagram, used once there to show a stretch. Expectations has two views, all scopes and compare two, switched with tabs; the top nav has five items. The theme follows the system unless someone uses the toggle.

## Content conventions

Scope files (`content/scopes/*.md`) have frontmatter (`id`, `name`, `order`, `radius`, `reaches`, `mindset`, `question`, `v1`; `reaches` is the short `·`-separated list on the home page's scope strip) and these sections:

```markdown
## Summary
## What changes            (from the previous scope)
## Client & Delivery       (one per area, matching the area's name)
> The one-line core statement.
### Expectations           (four at most)
### Evidence
### Self-check
### Not yet
### Ways to contribute   (Org & Community only: this scope's examples to choose from)
## Ways to create impact   (optional; intro paragraph, then ### per way)
## Examples                (### Meets expectations, ### Below expectations: one paragraph each)
```

Area files (`content/areas/*.md`) have `id`, `name`, `key` (`cd`, `pt`, `oc`), `order` and `question`, then `## Summary`, `## Facets` with `###` per facet, and any further `##` sections, which render as prose.

Renaming a scope or area heading changes what the parser matches, and changing a scope or area `id` breaks links such as `#scope/team/client-delivery`. Treat both as breaking changes.

Pages: `home.md` is orientation, not mechanics: its lede and two short `##` sections (Your scope, How growth happens) are looked up by heading, so renaming one needs the matching change in `js/views/home.js`. `how-it-works.md` holds the mechanics and `teammates.md` the guidance for reviewers; both render as plain reading pages (`##` sections), and How it works sections are linkable as `#how-it-works/<slug>`. In How it works, the Scopes, Growing into the next scope and The cycle sections are drawn as visuals from their `- **Title**: text` lists (see `js/views/how.js`), so keep that format and those headings. `faq.md` groups questions: `##` is a group, `###` a question, and each question is linkable as `#faq/<slug>`. Scope sections are linkable as `#scope/<scope>/<area>`.
