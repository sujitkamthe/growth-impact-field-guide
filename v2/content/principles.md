## Our principles

- **Growth is self-directed**: You choose the direction, shape and pace of your growth. Sahaj is an open field: the guide shows paths people have already taken through it, not fences, and every path that creates impact is valued.
- **Clarity without hierarchy**: Sahaj has no titles, so growing means being trusted with problems that reach further. That trust comes from impact you sustain over time, not from effort, long hours or a role.
- **Transparency and trust**: With open conversations and open salaries, expectations have to be written down, visible and fair. Anyone on your team can review your expectations, whatever their scope, and a change to your scope or a rating always comes with its reasons.
- **Feedback is a habit**: Feedback is given when the moment happens, by anyone to anyone, whatever their scope. Nothing at your team check should surprise you, because all of it has been said to you before.
- **Honest reflection**: Your self-assessment shows what happened and what your feedback told you; it isn't a case for a rating or a raise. Claiming more than happened stops you learning, and stretching is safe, because a well-made attempt counts even when it falls short.

## How the guide fits

The principles say what we believe about growth; the rest of the guide is how we put them into practice. **Scopes** describe how far your impact reaches, from your own work to beyond the team, so growing means reaching further rather than climbing a ladder. **Areas** are where that impact happens: Client & Delivery, People & Team, and Org & Community.

The expectations at each scope are signs of what impact looks like there, not a checklist to complete. The question they help answer is whether we would trust you with a problem of that reach. [How it works](#how-it-works) explains the mechanics, and [Expectations](#expectations) lists what each scope expects.
