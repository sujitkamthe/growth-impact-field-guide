## Our principles

- **Growth is self-directed**: *You choose the direction, shape and pace of your growth.* Every path that creates impact is valued. You can grow through your strengths, going deep where you do your best work, and your team helps you see those strengths and makes room for your next challenge. Every path still crosses all three areas, because each of them is part of what's expected of everyone.
- **Clarity without hierarchy**: *Growing means being trusted with problems that reach further.* Sahaj has no titles, so scopes give that clarity: each describes how far impact is expected to reach, so you and your team can agree what to expect of you. That trust comes from impact you sustain over time, not from effort, long hours or a role: one good example shows you can, and doing it consistently, so that people come to depend on it, shows you do.
- **Transparency and trust**: *Expectations are written down, visible and fair.* With open conversations and open salaries, expectations have to be written down, visible and fair. Every team, account and office uses the same guide and the same self-assessment, so expectations mean the same thing in Pune as in London, even when two people agree different expectations for their own work. Anyone on your team can review your expectations, whatever their scope, and a change to your scope or a rating always comes with its reasons.
- **Feedback is a habit**: *Given in the moment, by anyone, about all your impact.* Feedback is given when the moment happens, by anyone to anyone, whatever their scope, and it covers all of your impact, not only the work you deliver. It says what went well, what should change and where you could take on more, and the people already doing well need it as much as anyone. Nothing at your team check should surprise you, because all of it has been said to you before.
- **Honest reflection**: *Show what happened, not a case for a rating.* Your self-assessment shows what happened and what your feedback told you; it isn't a case for a rating or a raise. Claiming more than happened stops you learning, and stretching is safe, because a well-made attempt counts even when it falls short.
