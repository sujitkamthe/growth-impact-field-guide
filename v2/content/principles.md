## Our principles

- **Growth is self-directed**: *You choose the direction, shape and pace of your growth.* You choose the direction, shape and pace of your growth. Sahaj is an open field: the guide shows paths people have already taken through it, not fences, and every path that creates impact is valued.
- **Clarity without hierarchy**: *Growing means being trusted with problems that reach further.* Sahaj has no titles, so growing means being trusted with problems that reach further. Scopes give that clarity: each describes how far impact is expected to reach, so you and your team can agree what to expect of you. That trust comes from impact you sustain over time, not from effort, long hours or a role: one good example shows you can, and doing it consistently, so that people come to depend on it, shows you do.
- **Transparency and trust**: *Expectations are written down, visible and fair.* With open conversations and open salaries, expectations have to be written down, visible and fair. Every team, account and office uses the same guide and the same self-assessment, so expectations mean the same thing in Pune as in London. Anyone on your team can review your expectations, whatever their scope, and a change to your scope or a rating always comes with its reasons.
- **Feedback is a habit**: *Given in the moment, by anyone to anyone.* Feedback is given when the moment happens, by anyone to anyone, whatever their scope. Nothing at your team check should surprise you, because all of it has been said to you before.
- **Honest reflection**: *Show what happened, not a case for a rating.* Your self-assessment shows what happened and what your feedback told you; it isn't a case for a rating or a raise. Claiming more than happened stops you learning, and stretching is safe, because a well-made attempt counts even when it falls short.
