## In short

You can grow by going deep. You don't have to become a consultant, a delivery lead or a people lead to grow at Sahaj, at any scope.

All three areas are part of what's expected of everyone, and within each you choose how you contribute, so a specialist usually contributes through their strength: growing people in their own domain, or building a capability other teams use. When a strength the team already relies on would do more with a different split of the work, a [specialist agreement](#how-it-works/specialist-agreements), at Extended scope and above, lets you hand one responsibility to a teammate and agree a stronger contribution through your strength instead. You earn an agreement through a strength; you can't claim one by naming a weakness, and it never removes an area or a facet.

Depth is recognised by what it changes and how far it reaches, not by the choice to specialise.

## Choosing how, and choosing whether

*You choose how you contribute in each area; handing over a responsibility needs a specialist agreement.*

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| You don't enjoy one activity, such as writing | You choose another way to contribute in the same area. No agreement is needed. |
| You want to build up a strength | Agree opportunities and support with your team, while your other responsibilities continue. |
| You have a strength the team relies on, which would do more if you handed over one responsibility | A specialist agreement: a named teammate accepts the responsibility, and you agree a stronger contribution through your strength. |
| You want to stop contributing in a whole area | The area stays part of what's expected of you. Agree a way to contribute that suits you better. |

Wanting to grow a strength doesn't raise the bar by itself. The stronger contribution comes with the agreement, because it is what makes the handover fair to the teammate who takes the responsibility on.

## What an agreement includes

*An agreement names your strength, the responsibility you hand over, the teammate who takes it, and the stronger contribution you will make.*

The stronger contribution starts from the next scope's expectations for your strength, not from every next-scope expectation: a specialist in technical craft doesn't also take on next-scope consulting. Turn it into work your teammates will be able to recognise. For someone at Extended who specialises in reliability, that might be:

> Establish an approach to reliability that the whole team uses, help teammates apply it without me, and stop the recurring failures it was introduced to prevent.

That is clearer than "work at Team scope in technology". At Wider, where there is no next scope, agree the outcome directly.

Whatever you hand over, some expectations stay with you: communicating progress and risks, treating people with respect, giving and acting on feedback, keeping your commitments, and doing your part of the responsibility you handed over. A specialist who hands over leading the client's planning still gives the estimates and risks that planning depends on, and still keeps to what is agreed there.

## What makes depth count

*Depth counts by its effect, so agree what exceptional looks like before the cycle starts.*

It is more than writing better code or knowing more frameworks. Exceptional depth looks like:

- solving problems that would otherwise need outside experts
- cutting significant cost or risk, such as infrastructure spend or a class of production incidents
- letting Sahaj take on work it couldn't take on before
- technical decisions that prevent large problems later

The same applies to depth in consulting or delivery ownership, judged by the same kind of effect.

## Moving up as a specialist

*Depth can take you to the next scope, as long as your own impact reaches that scope in all three areas.*

Your scope is raised when you show next-scope impact consistently in all three areas, and a specialist can show all three through their strength. Expertise that improves work beyond your own is Extended. When the whole team relies on it and can work in your domain without you, it is Team. When teams beyond yours shape how they decide around it, it is Wider. Expertise kept to yourself stays at your own work, however deep it is.

For a specialist moving from Extended to Team, the evidence might look like this:

| Area | Evidence for Team |
|---|---|
| Client & Delivery | Your technical judgment improves how the whole team builds and decides. |
| People & Team | Teammates apply your approach with their own judgment, without coming back to you each time. |
| Org & Community | You build or strengthen a capability, programme or tool beyond the project, and other people keep it going. |

Answering questions helpfully is expected of everyone, but on its own it isn't Team-scope impact in People & Team. If your depth reaches further than your impact in the other areas, the depth is recognised in your assessment, and your scope stays where it is until the rest reaches the next scope too.

## Specialising without shifting the load

*No one specialises at someone else's unacknowledged expense.*

Take a team of four:

| Person | Strength | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| A | Technology | Solves the hardest engineering problems |
| B | Delivery ownership | Gets complex work delivered, and keeps the client's commitments realistic |
| C | Consulting | Understands the client's problem and shapes the solution |
| D | All three | Resolves the conflicts between what is feasible, what was promised and what the client expects |

Nothing is unfair about A, B and C specialising. It becomes unfair when D quietly takes on everything that falls between their specialities, and only the specialists' work is recognised. So one rule applies to everyone:

<!-- callout -->
No one specialises at someone else's unacknowledged expense.

In practice:

- **Handovers are accepted, not assumed.** A responsibility moves only to a teammate who agrees to take it, and an all-rounder isn't the default person for every specialist's handover.
- **Integration is impact.** Resolving trade-offs between technology, delivery and the client is consulting and delivery ownership impact, and People & Team impact when it keeps the team working together. D's reviewers name it in D's evidence, not as ordinary teamwork.
- **Support counts on both sides.** If a specialist's work needs a teammate to translate the problem or coordinate delivery, that support is part of the picture when the specialist's impact is judged, and part of the teammate's impact too.
- **Enabling counts too.** If A's expertise makes D's work much easier, that counts in A's favour.

## Recognising depth and breadth

*Neither depth nor breadth earns more recognition automatically; what it changes does.*

| Way | What exceptional looks like |
|---|---|
| **Depth** | Solving problems few others can, or bringing rare expertise that changes what the team or Sahaj can do |
| **Breadth** | Handling complex, ambiguous work that spans technology, consulting and delivery, and making the trade-offs between them |
| **Making others effective** | Expertise, tools, practices or guidance that make other people and teams substantially better |

These are not roles or a ranking, and people move between them. None needs to become another to be recognised as exceptional.

Your scope describes how far your impact reaches; your assessment records how much it changed. Exceptional depth, exceptional breadth and work that makes other people effective are all recorded there, whether or not they lead to a raise, because a raise in scope isn't the only way Sahaj recognises what someone contributes. Breadth deserves more recognition when combining strengths creates more value, such as settling the conflicts between what is feasible, promised and expected; depth deserves more when it solves problems or creates capability that others can't. How recognition relates to pay is decided outside this guide.

## How this works for three people

- **A deep technical specialist at Extended** agrees to hand leading the client's planning to a teammate, and to build the team's approach to reliability instead. They still give the estimates and risks planning depends on, grow teammates through reviews and pairing in their domain, and contribute to Sahaj through a capability track. Their scope grows as the team, then teams beyond it, rely on their expertise and learn it.
- **A strong consultant who is a weaker engineer** has no agreement to make for craft, because craft is the baseline at every scope. They build well enough that their framing turns into working software without someone rescuing it, agree support with their team to strengthen it, and contribute more through consulting. Their scope grows as clients and teams beyond their own act on the problems they frame.
- **A delivery-focused person who rarely consults or mentors** is below expectations where consulting is missing, or where, from Extended on, nobody grows because of them. Finishing work on time is part of delivery ownership, not the whole of Client & Delivery. Closing those gaps comes first: a specialist agreement rests on a strength the team relies on, and it is no way around a missing facet.
