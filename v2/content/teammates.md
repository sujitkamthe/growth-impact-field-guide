## Who reviews

*Everyone on a team reviews everyone else, whatever their scope.*

Anyone can comment on someone's expectations and ask for changes, give them feedback through the cycle, and confirm, correct or add to their self-assessment at the team check. You don't need to be senior to spot an expectation that's missing, and the people a senior's work reaches, such as the teammates they help grow, often see that impact most clearly.

## Reviewing someone's expectations

<!-- group: At the start of the cycle | Review their expectations before the doc is agreed | doc -->
Start from the scope they chose, and work through these before the doc is agreed.

1. **Does the impact you've seen support the scope?**: say so plainly if you think it's too high or too low. A mismatch is corrected here, so the whole cycle is measured against the right scope.
2. **Is anything specific to their work written down?**: expectations beyond the guide, such as what this client or team needs from them, go in their doc under the area they belong to.
3. **Is any stretch or development goal concrete?**: everyone should be able to recognise when it's happening. If they have been at the same scope for a long time, or their scope is below what their experience suggests, check they have agreed a development goal.
4. **Does any agreed focus stand up?**: check the results they've already shown in that strength, that the greater contribution meets a real need and is concrete enough to recognise at the team check, and that the teammate taking on the handed-over responsibility chose it and has room for it. See [Agreeing a focus](#how-it-works/agreeing-a-focus).

<!-- more -->
Add your inputs as comments and ask for changes where an expectation is missing or unclear. The doc is agreed when you and the person can both point to what's expected.

## Giving feedback during the cycle

<!-- group: During the cycle | Give feedback they can write down | feedback -->
*Give feedback when the moment happens, and give each piece three things.*

- **What you saw**: the specific moment, so they can recall it.
- **What it changed**: for the team, the client or the work.
- **Which expectation it relates to**: the area, and the facet where there is one.

<!-- more -->
Each person gathers feedback from the people they work with and keeps their own notes of it, so your part is to give it in a form they can write down. Give it when the moment happens, because feedback saved up for the team check comes too late to act on, and when someone asks you for feedback, answer soon and specifically: "all good" gives them nothing to record.

> **When something went well**
>
> In Tuesday's design review you laid out two options for the refund flow with their costs, and the client decided in the meeting rather than a week later. Client & Delivery, consulting.

Constructive feedback has the same shape, and adds what you'd suggest trying instead, so the person knows what to do with it. Give it soon and in private, while the moment is still fresh for both of you.

> **When something could go better**
>
> When our new joiner asked you about the flaky checkout test on Monday, you fixed it yourself in ten minutes. It unblocked them, but they still don't know why it failed, and they were stuck on the same test again on Wednesday. Next time, could you pair with them on it, even if it takes longer? People & Team, growing others.

Feedback they note down this way is what their self-assessment starts from. Over the cycle, cover all three areas, not only the work they deliver, and give feedback to the people already doing well too; [Feedback](#feedback) explains how, with more examples.

## Taking part in the team check

<!-- group: At the team check | Confirm, correct or add, and look ahead | team -->
*Look at what they did first, then the rating, and for each area confirm, correct or add.*

The person can present their doc, or you can read it beforehand for context. Look at what they did first, then the rating. For each area, respond in one of three ways:

<!-- list -->
- **Confirm** what matches your experience of their work.
- **Correct** where you think they misread their feedback, with the example that shows it.
- **Add** what they left out, especially work that is easy to miss, such as reviews, onboarding help, problems they prevented, or an attempt they stayed with that didn't succeed for reasons outside their control.

If a self-assessment mentions work you didn't see, ask about it: "I didn't see this directly, can you tell me more?" works better than silent scepticism.

Then look ahead with them. Say where you haven't seen evidence yet, especially in an area or facet nobody has commented on, so it comes up while there's a whole cycle to act on it, and agree what changes next cycle, such as an opportunity, support or time, and who arranges it. If they have an agreed focus, check that the greater contribution is happening and that the arrangement is still sustainable for the teammate who took on the handed-over responsibility. [Feedback](#feedback/looking-ahead-at-each-team-check) has the steps.

## When someone's scope grows

*Someone's scope grows once their contribution consistently reaches the next scope in all three areas.*

At the team check, agree that someone's scope has grown when their contribution consistently reaches the next scope in all three areas and the people around them depend on it; a single strong instance isn't enough. It doesn't have to have started as an agreed stretch. First check they meet their current scope in all three areas, because the next scope builds on it, and remember that every area counts: a shortfall in Org & Community holds them back just as one in Client & Delivery does. With an agreed focus, judge each area against it, so their next-scope impact in People & Team can come through their strength. It has to be the person's own: work a teammate took on for them counts for the teammate. The next cycle's doc is then written at the new scope.

## Proposing a stretch

<!-- tips: When it comes up -->
*Suggest a stretch when you see someone already doing some of the next scope's work.*

A stretch doesn't have to start with the person. If you see them already doing some of the next scope's work, or they have been at the same scope for a long time, suggest one when you review their expectations or as you look ahead at the team check. People who are already doing well need a next challenge as much as anyone: ask what they're strong at and want to grow in, and suggest a responsibility they haven't had before. Name what you've seen, what taking it on would involve and what support they'll need. Usually they decide whether to take it on, and write it into their doc.

Tell them what you've seen that makes you think they're ready. If the conversation starts to feel like criticism of their current work, go back to what you've seen them do well.

When someone has been at the same scope for a long time, or their scope is below what their experience suggests, talk with them about whether their work still challenges them. Find out why first: if there has been no opportunity, the team needs to make one; if support has been missing, give it; and sometimes nobody has asked them. Then agree a development goal with them, and the opportunities and support it needs, so their growth has a plan. The goal can deepen their contribution at their current scope or prepare them for the next one.

## When you disagree

*Disagreements usually mean you saw different slices of someone's work.*

Name the difference and the examples behind it; there is no vote, and the aim is to understand the pattern. Discuss a disagreement in private, and if it changes a scope or a rating, tell the person why. If the person and the people reviewing their work still disagree after that, about the scope or a rating, the team's seniors make a judgement call, leaving out the person whose doc it is. If that doesn't settle it, the account lead decides, or, for an account lead's own doc, a founder or the region's managing director.
