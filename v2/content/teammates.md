## Who reviews

*Anyone on the team can take part in a teammate's cycle, whatever their scope; each person asks the teammates who know their work.*

Anyone can comment on someone's expectations and ask for changes, give them feedback through the cycle, and confirm, correct or add to their self-assessment at the team check. You don't need to be senior to spot an expectation that's missing, and the people a senior's work reaches, such as the teammates they help grow, often see that impact most clearly.

## Reviewing someone's expectations

<!-- group: At the start of the cycle | Read their doc before it's agreed | doc -->
*Read their doc against the scope they chose, using the four checks on [Agree your expectations](#agree/get-it-agreed).*

Add your inputs as comments and ask for changes where an expectation is missing or unclear. If they have been at the same scope for a long time, or their scope is below what their experience suggests, check they have agreed a development goal. The doc is agreed when you and the person can both point to what's expected.

## Giving feedback during the cycle

<!-- group: During the cycle | Give feedback they can act on | feedback -->
*Give feedback when the moment happens: what you saw and what it changed, and what you'd suggest instead when something could go better.*

Each person keeps their own notes of the feedback they get, so give it soon and specifically: "all good" gives them nothing to keep. Cover all three areas over the cycle, not only the work they deliver, and give feedback to the people already doing well too. [Feedback](#feedback) explains how, with examples.

## Taking part in the team check

<!-- group: At the team check | Confirm, correct or add, and look ahead | team -->
*Look at what they did first, then the rating, and for each area confirm, correct or add.*

The person can present their doc, or you can read it beforehand for context. Look at what they did first, then the rating. For each area, respond in one of three ways:

<!-- list -->
- **Confirm** what matches your experience of their work.
- **Correct** where you think they misread their feedback, with the example that shows it.
- **Add** what they left out, especially work that is easy to miss, such as reviews, onboarding help, problems they prevented, or an attempt they stayed with that didn't succeed for reasons outside their control.

If a self-assessment mentions work you didn't see, ask about it: "I didn't see this directly, can you tell me more?" works better than silent scepticism.

Then look ahead with them. Say where you haven't seen evidence yet, especially in an area or facet nobody has commented on, so it comes up while there's a whole cycle to act on it, and agree what changes next cycle, such as an opportunity, support or time, and who arranges it. If they have an [agreed focus](#specialisation), check that the greater contribution is happening and that it is still sustainable for the teammate who took on the work. [Feedback](#feedback/looking-ahead-at-each-team-check) has the steps.

## When someone's scope grows

*Someone's scope grows once their contribution consistently reaches the next scope in all three areas.*

Use the rule in [Growing into the next scope](#how-it-works/growing-into-the-next-scope). Check first that they meet their current scope in all three areas, because the next scope builds on it, and count only their own work: work a teammate took on for them counts for the teammate. A shortfall in Org & Community holds them back just as one in Client & Delivery does.

## Proposing a stretch

<!-- tips: When it comes up -->
*Suggest a stretch when you see someone already doing some of the next scope's work.*

A stretch doesn't have to start with the person. If you see them already doing some of the next scope's work, or they have been at the same scope for a long time, suggest one when you review their expectations or as you look ahead at the team check. People who are already doing well need a next challenge as much as anyone: ask what they're strong at and want to grow in, and suggest a responsibility they haven't had before. Name what you've seen, what taking it on would involve and what support they'll need. Usually they decide whether to take it on, and write it into their doc.

Tell them what you've seen that makes you think they're ready. If the conversation starts to feel like criticism of their current work, go back to what you've seen them do well.

When someone has been at the same scope for a long time, find out why before agreeing a development goal with them: if there has been no opportunity, the team needs to make one; if support has been missing, give it; and sometimes nobody has asked them.

## When you disagree

*Disagreements usually mean you saw different slices of someone's work.*

Name the difference and the examples behind it; there is no vote, and the aim is to understand the pattern. Discuss a disagreement in private, and if it changes a scope or a rating, tell the person why. If you still disagree after that, [Who reviews and decides](#how-it-works/who-reviews-and-decides) says what happens next.
