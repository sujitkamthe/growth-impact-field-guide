## Before you write

<!-- group: Before you write | Your scope and what it expects | scope -->
<!-- row -->
1. **Confirm your scope**: the one you've shown consistently across all three areas. New to Sahaj? Agree your first scope with your team from your previous work.
2. **Read your scope's page**: each line says what impact looks like at your scope. They're signs to look for, not a checklist.
3. **Copy the template**: download it from the top of this page, and copy it into Google Drive.

## Write your doc

<!-- group: Write your doc | A line or two per row | doc -->
*Write only what this cycle needs from you beyond what your scope already expects; the expectations themselves stay in the guide.*

| Row | What to write | From a finished doc, at Team scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Client & Delivery** | What this client and project need from you: a stream you'll own, a decision the client needs help with, a commitment you'll keep realistic | Payment releases have slipped twice because integrations are tested late. Work with the team on how we plan, test and review integrations, so the next two releases ship on the dates we agree with the client. |
| **People & Team** | Who you'll help grow and how, or what the team needs from you, such as onboarding a new joiner | Pair with a newer teammate on contract tests until they can design them on their own, and hand the integration review to two teammates. |
| **Org & Community** | How you'll contribute beyond your project, in a way you choose, and when you'll make the time | With two teammates, rebuild how the office onboards engineers into testing, and hand it over to them to run. |
| **Growth** | A stretch into some of the next scope's work, or a strength you want to deepen; if this cycle is about settling in, say that | Deepen my integration testing practice to the point where another team on the account asks to use it. |

<!-- more: When the ordinary doc doesn't fit -->
The same person's self-assessment at the end of this cycle is on [Self-assessment](#self-assessment/a-worked-example). Two arrangements are for when the ordinary doc doesn't fit: if you've already shown real depth in a strength and want to make it your main contribution, see [Agreed focus](#specialisation); if your project leaves no room for Org & Community, see [How it is assessed](#area/org-community). Most docs need neither.

## Get it agreed

<!-- group: Get it agreed | Share it with the teammates who know your work | team -->
*Share the doc with the teammates who know your work across the three areas. Before it's agreed, they check:*

<!-- checks -->
- **Your scope fits what they've seen**: if it looks too high or too low, it's corrected now, so the whole cycle is measured against the right one.
- **What's specific to your work is written down**: what this client or team needs from you, under its row.
- **Any stretch or development goal is concrete**: everyone should be able to recognise when it's happening.
- **Any agreed focus stands up**: see [Agreed focus](#specialisation).

<!-- more: When it's agreed, and changing it later -->
The doc is agreed when you and they can both point to what's expected. If you can't agree, the team's seniors help settle it, as described in [Who reviews and decides](#how-it-works/who-reviews-and-decides). When circumstances change during the cycle, such as a new client priority, change the doc then, so your self-assessment measures against what was actually expected.
