title: Agree your expectations
lede: At the start of each cycle, agree with your team what's expected of you, and write it in a short doc. Follow the three steps; a finished doc is at the end.
---

## Before you write

<!-- group: Before you write | Your scope and what it expects | scope -->
1. **Confirm your scope**: the one you've shown consistently across all three areas. If you're new to Sahaj, agree your first scope with your team from the impact you had in your previous work.
2. **Read your scope's page**: each line says what impact looks like at your scope. They are signs to look for, not a checklist to complete.
3. **Make a copy of the template**: one page, with a row per area. Download [the expectations doc](doc-templates/expectations.docx).

## Write your doc

<!-- group: Write your doc | A line or two per area, and where you want to grow | doc -->
*Write only what this cycle needs from you beyond what your scope already expects; the expectations themselves stay in the guide.*

- **Client & Delivery**: what this client and project need from you, such as a stream you'll own, a decision the client needs help with, or a commitment you'll keep realistic.
- **People & Team**: who you'll help grow and how, or what the team needs from you, such as onboarding a new joiner.
- **Org & Community**: how you'll contribute beyond your project this cycle, in a way you choose, and when you'll make the time.
- **Growth**: a stretch into some of the next scope's work, or a strength you want to deepen. If this cycle is about settling in, say that.

<!-- more -->
Two arrangements are for when the ordinary doc doesn't fit. If you want to take less of a particular responsibility and contribute more through a strength, see [Agreed focus](#specialisation). If your project leaves no room for Org & Community, agree it as described in [How it is assessed](#area/org-community). Most docs need neither.

## Get it agreed

<!-- group: Get it agreed | Share it with the teammates who know your work | team -->
Share the doc with the teammates who know your work across the three areas, and ask them to read it before it's agreed. They check four things:

1. **Does the impact they've seen support your scope?**: if they think it's too high or too low, they say so, and the scope is corrected now, so the whole cycle is measured against the right one.
2. **Is what's specific to your work written down?**: what this client or team needs from you goes in the doc, under its area.
3. **Is any stretch or development goal concrete?**: everyone should be able to recognise when it's happening.
4. **Does any agreed focus stand up?**: see [Agreed focus](#specialisation).

<!-- more -->
The doc is agreed when you and they can both point to what's expected. If you can't agree, the team's seniors help settle it, as described in [Who reviews and decides](#how-it-works/who-reviews-and-decides). When circumstances change during the cycle, such as a new client priority, change the doc then, so your self-assessment measures against what was actually expected.

## A finished doc

<!-- tips: If you need it -->
*A doc for someone at Team scope. Their self-assessment at the end of the same cycle is on [Self-assessment](#self-assessment/a-worked-example).*

> **Scope**: Team
>
> **Client & Delivery**: Payment releases have slipped twice because integrations are tested late. Work with the team on how we plan, test and review integrations, so the next two releases ship on the dates we agree with the client.
>
> **People & Team**: Pair with a newer teammate on contract tests until they can design them on their own, and hand the integration review to two teammates by the end of the cycle.
>
> **Org & Community**: With two teammates, rebuild how the office onboards engineers into testing, and hand it over to them to run.
>
> **Growth**: Deepen my integration testing practice to the point where another team on the account asks to use it.
