## Summary

Your impact changes how the whole team operates, performs and grows, rather than improving one part of its work: its architecture and design, how its people develop, and whether it is a high-performing team. Your leverage comes from people, systems and standards rather than from your presence, and the real test is what holds when you step away.

## What changes

At Extended you improve work beyond your own. At Team you improve the system that produces the work: the architecture, the design bar, how people grow and how the team performs. A useful test is whether the team would keep working differently if you stopped touching individual stories tomorrow.

## Client & Delivery

> I own the team's architecture, design and delivery, not just a part of its work.

### Expectations

- Owns the team's architecture and technical design, and sets standards the team follows without needing enforcement
- Leads a team or a one-team account, or bootstraps one, owning delivery health, the technical bar and consulting quality
- Is the voice when things are going wrong at the client or at Sahaj, raising risks and driving them to actionable outcomes
- Supports proposals and demand conversations, and brings deep expertise in at least one area

### Evidence

- The team making good decisions while you were away, more than once
- Architecture or design decisions the team still builds on
- Practices or standards still in use after you stopped pushing them
- Risks you raised that changed course, and proposals or demand conversations you contributed to

### Self-check

- If I stepped away for a month, what would break and what would hold?
- Does the client trust the team, or does everything still route through me?

### Not yet

- The single point of contact for every decision
- Standards that hold only while you enforce them

## People & Team

> I build a high-performing team that grows its own people.

### Expectations

- Develops people so they take on new responsibilities, and coaches others to mentor
- Builds a high-performing team: notices team dynamics and improves them, and builds learning into how the team works
- Mentors and coaches beyond the team, within the wider organisation
- Keeps the team sustainable, preventing burnout rather than managing it afterwards

### Evidence

- People who grew into new responsibilities over time, and what you did to help
- Team rituals or forums that run without you

### Self-check

- Is the team stronger when I step away?
- Do I invest in the people who need it most, or only in the ones who ask?

### Not yet

- Delegates tasks without transferring context or judgment
- Mentors individuals while the team's overall capability stays flat

## Org & Community

> I execute org initiatives and help build the organisation.

### Expectations

- Executes org initiatives end to end and brings others along with them
- Takes part in building the organisation: hiring, onboarding, office life and capability
- Speaks up when things are not going in the right direction at Sahaj, and helps fix them

### Evidence

- An initiative delivered end to end, its outcome, and who carried it with you
- Cultural or organisational problems you named and helped fix, and whether they stayed fixed

### Self-check

- Would what I built keep running if I stopped tomorrow?

### Not yet

- Initiatives that fade as soon as you move on

## Ways to create impact

There is more than one way to reach this scope. None of them is the easier path: each still needs People & Team and Org & Community impact, expressed through a different mechanism.

### Leading the team

You scale through people, delivery and client trust. The team's capability is the output, and your own contribution matters less each quarter.

### Platforms, tools and standards

You scale through things the team, and often other teams, choose to adopt. Nobody is assigned to use them, so the bar on consulting and communication is higher, not lower: the artefact has to solve a real problem and explain itself. This was the Amplifier persona in V1.

### Deep expertise

You become the person the team relies on in a hard domain, and you make that expertise transferable rather than a bottleneck.

## Examples

### Meets expectations

The team made most technical decisions without you, and when you took two weeks off, pace and quality held. The architecture you set is what the team builds on, and the review practice you introduced continued after you stopped running it. Two teammates now lead client conversations because you paired with them and gradually stepped back. You also carried an onboarding improvement for the office from idea to rollout.

### Below expectations

You led the team, but every technical decision and client conversation went through you, and the team waited when you were away. You mentored individuals, yet the team's capability didn't move: people escalated the same problems they did six months earlier. The practices you introduced were followed only while you enforced them.
