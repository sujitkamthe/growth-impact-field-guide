## Summary

Your impact reaches past the features you own, to work that spans features or belongs to other people. You shape how pieces fit together, lead a stream of related features, and other people's work goes better because you were involved.

## What changes

At Direct you are responsible for your own stories and features. At Extended your influence reaches work you are not building yourself, such as a design others implement, a stream of features, or a teammate you help unblock.

## Client & Delivery

> I shape work beyond my own features, and help the team and client decide what the right solution is.

### Expectations

- Leads a stream or a cross-cutting feature end to end, or bootstraps a new one: design, plan, dependencies and delivery risk
- Designs for needs that span features, anticipating integration problems, failure modes and technical debt before they bite
- Reframes vague asks into clear problems, proposes options and makes trade-offs explicit for stakeholders
- Advocates for Sahaj's delivery approach and persuades the client team to do the right thing, including pushing back when an ask is risky

### Evidence

- Streams or cross-cutting features you led, and how they went
- Designs other people built their features on without needing you
- Vague asks you reframed, and whether the team now brings ambiguous work to you
- Times you pushed back with the client, and what changed as a result

### Self-check

- Is my impact visible in features I didn't build myself?
- Do stakeholders come to me for thinking, or only for updates?
- When I was away for a week, did my stream keep moving?

### Not yet

- Excellent work on your own features, with little effect on anyone else's
- Contributes solutions in design discussions but leaves the framing to someone else
- Acts as the hub everyone routes decisions through, so things stall during absences

## People & Team

> People working on other features become more capable because of my involvement.

### Expectations

- Mentors at least one teammate consistently, helping them build their own judgment
- Helps others succeed on their features through design reviews, pairing and unblocking, not by taking the work over
- Gives timely feedback that changes behaviour, including feedback that is hard to give
- Drives discussions to decisions and handles disagreement without escalating

### Evidence

- Someone whose judgment grew with your mentoring over the cycle, ideally in their words
- Teammates' features that went better because you reviewed or paired, and whether they now seek you out
- Feedback you gave that led to lasting change

### Self-check

- Can I name someone who now handles something independently because of me?
- Am I building independence, or dependence on me?

### Not yet

- Answers questions without teaching the reasoning behind them
- Helps by taking the work over rather than helping the person grow

## Org & Community

> I drive something beyond my project, not just take part in it.

### Expectations

- Owns or meaningfully drives an org initiative, community or capability effort
- Contributes to the community inside and outside Sahaj, for example through sessions, writing, talks or open source
- Advocates for our culture and values, and calls out what doesn't fit them

### Evidence

- The initiative you drove through the cycle, what you did and what changed
- Something you created that others keep using

### Self-check

- Would this initiative have moved without me?
- Did anyone outside my project benefit?

### Not yet

- Being one of many participants is described as driving
- A talk or a post with no follow-through

## Examples

### Meets expectations

You led the stream for a new payments flow. You turned a vague ask into a clear problem, laid out two options with their trade-offs, and three teammates built their features on your design without needing you. You paired regularly with a newer teammate, who now handles similar design work alone. You also drove the office's DevDay planning through the cycle.

### Below expectations

Your own features were excellent, but your effect on anyone else's work was small. In design discussions you offered solutions and left the framing to someone else. People routed decisions through you because it was faster, and two of them stalled while you were on leave. Your contribution beyond delivery was one talk with no follow-up.
