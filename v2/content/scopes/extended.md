## Summary

Your impact reaches past the features you own, to work that spans features or belongs to other people. You shape how pieces fit together, lead a stream of related features, and other people's work goes better because you were involved.

## What changes

At Direct you are responsible for your own stories and features. At Extended your influence reaches work you are not building yourself, such as a design others implement, a stream of features, or a teammate you help unblock.

## In practice

A client asks the team to add a second payment provider to its checkout. Payment changes have slipped in each of the last two releases.

- **What you did**: You saw that checkout, refunds and reporting would all need changes, so you designed how the new provider fits across them, and turned "add a provider" into a clear problem with two options for the client.
- **What changed**: Three teammates built their parts on your design without waiting for you, and the client chose an option knowing its trade-offs.
- **Why it's Extended**: Your impact reached work other people built. Building only your own part well would be Direct; changing how the team handles every integration would be Team.

## Client & Delivery

> I shape work beyond my own features, and help the team and client decide what the right solution is.

### What changes

At Direct you deliver your own stories well; at Extended you shape work that spans features and other people.

### Expectations

#### Technical craft

- Designs solutions for complex or cross-cutting requirements, making deliberate trade-offs across performance, cost and reliability
- Anticipates edge cases, failure modes and technical debt, improving test strategy and observability for the work they shape, before problems surface

#### Consulting

- Reframes vague asks into clear problems, separating symptoms from root causes, and proposes options with their trade-offs
- Acts as a thought partner rather than an order-taker, pushing back on misaligned or risky asks while keeping trust

#### Delivery

- Owns a stream or epic of work, including its cross-feature dependencies and delivery risk
- Makes scope, time and quality trade-offs explicit, and improves how work flows across their stream

### Evidence

- Streams, cross-cutting features or new work you shaped and coordinated, and how they went
- Designs other people built their features on without needing you
- Vague asks you reframed, and whether the team now brings ambiguous work to you
- Times you pushed back with the client, and what changed as a result

### Self-check

- Is my impact visible in features I didn't build myself?
- Do stakeholders come to me for thinking, or only for updates?
- Have I shared enough context that my stream can move without me?
- Do I push back on misaligned requirements, or accept them to avoid conflict?

### Not yet

- Excellent work on your own features, with little effect on anyone else's
- Contributes solutions in design discussions but leaves the framing to someone else
- Acts as the hub everyone routes decisions through, so things stall during absences

## People & Team

> People working on other features become more capable because of my involvement.

### What changes

At Direct you are a dependable teammate; at Extended other people's work and judgment improve because of you.

### Expectations

#### Growing others

- Mentors consistently over time, so others build judgment of their own
- Helps others succeed on their features through design reviews, pairing and unblocking, without taking the work over

#### Feedback and trust

- Gives timely, specific feedback on things that matter, including hard feedback, and follows it up
- Resolves conflicts constructively, so the people involved keep working well together

#### Communication and collaboration

- Facilitates discussions and workshops to clear decisions, summarising options and next steps
- Handles disagreement constructively, influencing through clear reasoning, and escalating only when that fails

### Evidence

- Someone whose judgment grew with your mentoring over the cycle, and what they now handle without you
- Teammates' features that went better because you reviewed or paired, and whether they now seek you out
- Feedback you gave and followed up, and the decisions or behaviour that improved as a result
- Discussions you facilitated that ended in a decision people acted on

### Self-check

- Can I name someone who now handles something independently because of me?
- Am I building independence, or dependence on me?

### Not yet

- Answers questions without teaching the reasoning behind them
- Helps by taking the work over, so the person doesn't grow
- The go-to person for doing the work, rather than for making others better at it

## Org & Community

> I drive something beyond my project, not just take part in it.

### What changes

At Direct you take part in something beyond your project; at Extended you drive it and carry it through.

### Expectations

- Takes responsibility for a piece of org work that matters beyond their project, and carries it through
- Creates or improves something that people beyond their project benefit from

### Evidence

- The initiative you drove through the cycle, what you did, and what changed or what got in the way
- Something you created or improved that people outside your project keep using
- A problem you raised, and what happened next

### Self-check

- Would this initiative have moved without me?
- Did anyone outside my project benefit?

### Not yet

- Being one of many participants is described as driving
- A talk or a post with no follow-through

### Ways to contribute

#### Culture and organisational health

- Raising a cultural or organisational problem constructively and working on the fix

#### Initiatives and capability

- Owning part of an org initiative or capability track and carrying it through
- Improving part of hiring, such as an interview exercise or how feedback is written
- Building or improving an internal tool that people beyond your project use
- Mentoring through a programme and helping improve how it runs

#### Community and external contribution

- Running a community of practice or a regular learning forum
- Writing or speaking about your work, and following up with the people it reached

## Examples

### Meets expectations

You led the stream for a new payments flow. You turned a vague ask into a clear problem, laid out two options with their trade-offs, and three teammates built their features on your design without needing you. You paired regularly with a newer teammate, who now handles similar design work alone. You also took responsibility for rebuilding the office's interview exercise and carried it through to the version interviewers now use.

### Below expectations

Your own features were excellent, but your effect on anyone else's work was small. In design discussions you offered solutions and left the framing to someone else. People routed decisions through you because it was faster, and two of them stalled while you were on leave. Your contribution beyond delivery was one talk with no follow-up.
