## Summary

Your impact reaches past the features you own, to work that spans features or belongs to other people. You shape how pieces fit together, lead a stream of related features, and other people's work goes better because you were involved.

## What changes

At Direct you are responsible for your own stories and features. At Extended your influence reaches **work you are not building yourself**, such as a design others implement, a stream of features, or a teammate you help unblock.

## In practice

A client asks the team to add a second payment provider to its checkout. Payment changes have slipped in each of the last two releases.

- **What you did**: You saw that checkout, refunds and reporting would all need changes, so you designed how the new provider fits across them, and turned "add a provider" into a clear problem with two options for the client.
- **What changed**: Three teammates built their parts on your design without waiting for you, and the client chose an option knowing its trade-offs.
- **Why it's Extended**: Your impact reached work other people built. Building only your own part well would be Direct; changing how the team handles every integration would be Team.

## Client & Delivery

> I shape work beyond my own features, and help the team and client decide what the right solution is.

### What changes

At Direct you deliver your own stories well; at Extended you shape work that **spans features and other people**.

### Expectations

#### Technical craft

- **Design cross-cutting solutions**: I design solutions for **complex or cross-cutting requirements**, making deliberate trade-offs across performance, cost and reliability
  - Design how a requirement that **spans several features** fits across them, so the people building each part work from one design
  - Weigh performance, cost and reliability **on purpose**, and explain why I chose the trade-off I did
  - Go through the design **with the people who will build on it** before they start
- **Catch problems early**: I find edge cases, failure modes and technical debt in **the work I shape** before they cause problems
  - Look for edge cases and failure modes **while designing**, not after release
  - **Name the technical debt** a design creates, and agree with the team when it will be paid down
  - Improve the **tests and monitoring** for the work I shape, so the next problem is caught sooner

#### Consulting

- **Reframe vague asks**: I **turn vague asks into clear problems**, and offer options with their trade-offs
  - Separate **symptoms from root causes** before proposing a solution
  - Write the problem down in **words the client agrees with**
  - Offer the client **more than one option**, with what each costs and risks, so they decide knowing the trade-offs
- **Push back with reasons**: I **push back on misaligned or risky asks** and explain why, without losing the client's trust
  - **Say early** when an ask works against the client's goals or carries a risk they haven't seen
  - Explain my reasoning and **offer an alternative**, rather than only saying no
  - **Keep working well with the client** after a disagreement, whichever way the decision goes

#### Delivery

- **Own a stream**: I **own a stream or epic** of work, including its dependencies on other features and its delivery risk
  - Own the stream **from start to finish**, so the team and client know to come to me about it
  - Track its **dependencies on other features and teams**, and sort them out before they block anyone
  - Raise its **delivery risks** early and drive each one to a decision
- **Start up a new stream**: I **set up new streams** so the people on them can start delivering quickly
  - Shape the **first backlog**, so the people joining have clear work from the first week
  - Agree with the team how the work will be done and **who works on what**
  - Bring the people joining **up to speed** on the client and the problem
- **Make trade-offs explicit**: I make trade-offs between features, time and quality **explicit** to the team and client
  - Tell the team and client **what each choice gives up**, instead of absorbing it quietly
  - Improve **how work moves through my stream**, removing delays I see repeat

### Evidence

- Streams, cross-cutting features or new work you shaped and coordinated, and how they went
- Designs other people built their features on without needing you
- Vague asks you reframed, and whether the team now brings ambiguous work to you
- Times you pushed back with the client, and what changed as a result

### Self-check

- Is my impact visible in features I didn't build myself?
- Do stakeholders come to me for thinking, or only for updates?
- Have I shared enough context that my stream can move without me?
- Do I push back on misaligned requirements, or accept them to avoid conflict?

### Not yet

- Excellent work on your own features, with little effect on anyone else's
- Contributes solutions in design discussions but leaves the framing to someone else
- Acts as the hub everyone routes decisions through, so things stall during absences

## People & Team

> People working on other features become more capable because of my involvement.

### What changes

At Direct you are a dependable teammate; at Extended **other people's** work and judgment improve because of you.

### Expectations

#### Growing others

- **Mentor over time**: I **mentor consistently over time**, so the people I mentor build judgment of their own
  - Meet the people I mentor **regularly through the cycle**, not only when they ask
  - Explain the **reasoning behind my advice**, so they can make the next call without me
  - Give them **harder work** as their judgment grows
- **Help without taking over**: I help teammates succeed **on their features**, without taking the work over
  - Review designs and pair with teammates **on features I'm not building**, when it helps them
  - Unblock them by showing how, and **leave the work in their hands**

#### Feedback and trust

- **Give hard feedback and follow it up**: I give timely, specific feedback on things that matter, **including hard feedback, and follow it up**
  - Give feedback **soon after the moment**, with a specific example
  - Give **hard feedback** rather than avoiding it, and check later whether it helped
- **Resolve conflicts**: I **resolve conflicts constructively**, so the people involved keep working well together
  - **Raise a conflict early** with the people involved, rather than talking around them
  - Help them **agree on a way forward** they can both work with

#### Communication and collaboration

- **Run discussions to a decision**: I **facilitate discussions and workshops** that end in a clear decision
  - **Prepare the options** and what needs deciding before the discussion
  - **Summarise the decision and next steps**, and share them with everyone affected
- **Influence through reasoning**: I **handle disagreement constructively**, influencing through clear reasoning
  - Make my case with **evidence and reasoning**, and listen to the reasoning of the people who disagree
  - **Escalate only** when reasoning hasn't settled it, and say openly that I am escalating

### Evidence

- Someone whose judgment grew with your mentoring over the cycle, and what they now handle without you
- Teammates' features that went better because you reviewed or paired, and whether they now seek you out
- Feedback you gave and followed up, and the decisions or behaviour that improved as a result
- Discussions you facilitated that ended in a decision people acted on

### Self-check

- Can I name someone who now handles something independently because of me?
- Am I building independence, or dependence on me?

### Not yet

- Answers questions without teaching the reasoning behind them
- Helps by taking the work over, so the person doesn't grow
- The go-to person for doing the work, rather than for making others better at it

## Org & Community

> I drive something beyond my project, not just take part in it.

### What changes

At Direct you take part in something beyond your project; at Extended you **drive it and carry it through**.

### Expectations

- **Drive an org initiative**: I **take responsibility for a piece of org work** that matters beyond my project, and carry it through
  - Take on a **defined piece of an org initiative**, with an outcome people can check
  - **Keep it moving** through the cycle, and say early when it is stuck
  - **Hand it over or close it properly**, rather than letting it fade
- **Build something others use**: I create or improve something that **people beyond my project** benefit from
  - Find out what the **people beyond my project** need before I build it
  - **Keep improving it** from their feedback, so they keep using it

### Evidence

- The initiative you drove through the cycle, what you did, and what changed or what got in the way
- Something you created or improved that people outside your project keep using
- A problem you raised, and what happened next

### Self-check

- Would this initiative have moved without me?
- Did anyone outside my project benefit?

### Not yet

- Being one of many participants is described as driving
- A talk or a post with no follow-through

### Ways to contribute

#### Culture and organisational health

- Raising a cultural or organisational problem constructively and working on the fix

#### Initiatives and capability

- Owning part of an org initiative or capability track and carrying it through
- Building or improving an internal tool that people beyond your project use
- Mentoring through a programme and helping improve how it runs

#### Community and external contribution

- Running a community of practice or a regular learning forum
- Writing a blog post or giving a conference talk about your work, and following up with the people it reached
- Organising a DevDay and carrying it through, from choosing the speakers to following up afterwards

## Examples

### Meets expectations

You led the stream for a new payments flow. You turned a vague ask into a clear problem, laid out two options with their trade-offs, and three teammates built their features on your design without needing you. You paired regularly with a newer teammate, who now handles similar design work alone. You also took on organising the office's DevDay and carried it through, from choosing the speakers to following up on what teams said they would try.

### Below expectations

Your own features were excellent, but your effect on anyone else's work was small. In design discussions you offered solutions and left the framing to someone else. People routed decisions through you because it was faster, and two of them stalled while you were on leave. Your contribution beyond delivery was one talk with no follow-up.
