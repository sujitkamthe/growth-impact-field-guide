## Summary

Your impact reaches past the team you work in. For most people that means an account with several teams, or the client's organisation as a whole; it can also mean Sahaj itself or the wider community. These sit side by side rather than one above another, and you are not expected to cover all of them: a person with deep influence inside one client's organisation, or on capability across Sahaj, meets this scope as fully as someone known in the wider community. What matters is that your impact lands somewhere beyond one team, is sustained, and outlasts your involvement. One talk, one DevDay or one blog post is not Wider impact on its own; a capability adopted across Sahaj, changed engineering practice across a client's organisation, or a multi-team account run well can be.

## What changes

At Team you change how one team operates. At Wider your impact crosses the team boundary, into a multi-team account, the client's organisation, Sahaj or the wider community, and it lasts beyond your involvement.

## Client & Delivery

> I shape the client's direction, not only the delivery.

### Expectations

- Leads a multi-team account, or grows an account into one, owning its health and growth beyond any one team's delivery
- Consults at the account level: shapes architecture and technology roadmaps, prioritises larger initiatives and makes hard-to-reverse decisions responsibly
- Influences senior stakeholders and acts as a change agent for the client's organisation design and culture
- Leads proposals and demand conversations

### Evidence

- Roadmaps or strategic decisions you shaped, and how they played out over time
- Senior stakeholders who repeatedly seek your counsel on strategic questions
- Proposals you led, or an account that grew because of how you ran it

### Self-check

- Will these decisions still look right in a few years?
- Do senior clients seek my judgment, or my team's output?

### Not yet

- Strategy lives in decks and doesn't change what teams do
- The client relationship depends on you personally rather than on how the account is run

## People & Team

> I grow people and leaders beyond my own team.

### Expectations

- Sponsors high-potential people into opportunities they would not otherwise reach
- Is a role model across Sahaj, and often beyond it
- Builds people capability that outlasts their own involvement

### Evidence

- People you sponsored, and where they are now
- Leaders who grew while working with you

### Self-check

- Am I building capability that will outlast me?

### Not yet

- Mentors the same few people they would have mentored anyway

## Org & Community

> I shape how Sahaj grows, how it works, or how it is seen.

### Expectations

- Designs org initiatives rather than only running them: sees what Sahaj needs and shapes the response
- Creates durable impact on Sahaj's capability, culture or reputation, through internal work or external influence; external work counts when it changes how people work, not for its visibility
- Shapes and protects our culture, especially under pressure

### Evidence

- Initiatives you designed, and their effect beyond your own office or account
- Changes to how Sahaj, or the client's organisation, works that are still in use

### Self-check

- Will what I shaped still be creating value a year after I stop?

### Not yet

- Recognised outside Sahaj far more than relied on inside it

## Ways to create impact

Impact at this scope travels through different routes, and most people combine more than one. Whichever route you take, it has to show up across all three areas.

### Leading an account

You shape how Sahaj shows up at a client: the relationship, the roadmap and the demand pipeline.

### Technical direction

You set direction the account, or teams across Sahaj, align to, and you protect Sahaj's technical reputation.

### Capability and culture

You design how Sahaj builds capability, hires, onboards and looks after its culture.

### External thought leadership

You shape how the industry thinks through writing, talks and open source. Visibility without adoption is vanity, so this counts when it also changes how Sahaj works. This was the Pioneer persona in V1.

## Examples

### Meets expectations

You grew an account from one team to three and set the architecture and delivery approach all three follow. Senior client stakeholders bring you their strategic questions, and the roadmap they are now executing is one you shaped. You sponsored two engineers into leading teams on the account, and inside Sahaj you designed a capability track on the account's domain that other offices now use.

### Below expectations

You led the account well, but your impact stopped at its edge: strong decisions for your client, nothing shaped beyond it. The direction you set lived mostly in conversations and decks, teams interpreted it differently, and under delivery pressure it was quietly dropped.
