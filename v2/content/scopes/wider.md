## Summary

Your impact reaches past the team you work in. For most people that means the account as a whole, whether it has one team or several, or the client's organisation; it can also mean Sahaj itself or the wider community. These sit side by side rather than one above another, and you are not expected to cover all of them: a person with deep influence inside one client's organisation, or on capability across Sahaj, meets this scope as fully as someone known in the wider community. Your impact has to make a difference somewhere beyond one team and last after you step back. One talk, one DevDay or one blog post is not Wider impact on its own; a capability adopted across Sahaj, changed engineering practice across a client's organisation, or an account grown and kept healthy can be.

## What changes

At Team you change how one team operates. At Wider your impact **crosses the team boundary**, into the account as a whole, the client's organisation, Sahaj or the wider community, and it lasts beyond your involvement.

## In practice

A client asks the team to add a second payment provider to its checkout. Payment changes have slipped in each of the last two releases.

- **What you did**: You found that three of the client's teams were integrating payment providers separately and hitting the same problems. You helped the client's leadership decide on one shared approach, and brought the teams to it.
- **What changed**: The client consolidated its providers on a roadmap you shaped, and its teams stopped solving the same problem three times.
- **Why it's Wider**: Your impact crossed the team boundary into the client's organisation and outlasted your involvement. Fixing it inside your own team would be Team.

## Client & Delivery

> I shape outcomes beyond one team, not only the delivery.

### What changes

At Team you change how one team works; at Wider you shape direction **across teams or a client's organisation**.

### Expectations

#### Technical craft

- **Shape architecture beyond one team**: I shape long-term architecture **beyond one team**, making hard-to-reverse decisions responsibly
  - Shape architecture that **several teams, or a client's wider systems**, depend on
  - Keep **the systems different teams build fitting together**, so they don't drift into designs that clash
  - Involve the people affected in decisions that are **hard to reverse**, weigh the options and record why we chose
  - Shape **the account's technical roadmap**, and help the client prioritise its larger technical initiatives
- **Anticipate technology shifts**: I **anticipate technology and industry shifts**, and invest in Sahaj's long-term technical capability
  - Follow changes in technology and the industry, and judge **which ones matter to our clients**
  - Help Sahaj build **the skills it will need** before clients ask for them
  - Build **Sahaj's capability in AI-assisted delivery**, such as practices, harnesses and training that teams across Sahaj adopt and keep using

#### Consulting

- **Advise senior stakeholders**: I **earn the trust of senior stakeholders**, at a client or across Sahaj, and advise them on multi-year, multi-stakeholder problems
  - **Senior stakeholders**, at a client or in Sahaj, come to me for advice on problems that span years and many groups
  - Give them **advice they can act on**, including when it isn't what they hoped to hear
- **Improve how Sahaj consults**: I **improve how Sahaj frames problems** and engages clients
  - Share how I frame problems and work with clients, so **people across Sahaj** can use it
  - Help **teams I don't lead** through hard client situations
- **Change how the client's organisation works**: I **help the client change how its organisation works**, such as how its teams are structured or its engineering culture
  - Show the client's leaders **where structure or culture slows delivery**, with evidence
  - Work with them on the change **until their teams work the new way**
  - Help the client decide **where AI belongs in their engineering organisation**, including their rules on data, code ownership and checking generated work
- **Lead proposals**: I **lead proposals and demand conversations**, from framing the client's problem to the approach and team we propose
  - Frame **the client's problem** before proposing a solution
  - Propose **only what Sahaj's teams can deliver well**, so winning the work doesn't set them up to struggle

#### Delivery ownership

- **Shape the account's direction**: I shape direction **for the account or across several teams**, or for part of a client's organisation, aligning technical, delivery and business priorities
  - Set direction for **the account as a whole**, for several teams, or for part of a client's organisation
  - Align **technical, delivery and business priorities**, so the teams don't pull against each other
- **Keep delivery healthy as it grows**: I keep delivery healthy as the work grows, fixing problems **before they spread across teams**
  - Watch **delivery across teams**, and notice problems that could spread
  - Fix them **before they spread**, and change what caused them
  - Agree **commitments across teams** with senior client stakeholders that the teams can keep, settling the conflicts between commercial, technical and delivery pressures that one team can't settle alone
- **Start up or grow an account**: I **set up a new account, or grow an existing one**, so its teams start and keep working well and the client relationship grows
  - Shape the **first engagement** with the client: the problem, the approach and the team
  - Set up **the first teams** and how they work with the client
  - Build the relationship with the client, so the account can grow **beyond its first team**
  - Look after **an existing account as a whole**: its teams, its delivery and the client relationship

### Evidence

- Roadmaps, strategic decisions or changes to how the client's organisation works that you shaped, and how they played out over time
- Senior stakeholders who repeatedly seek your counsel on strategic questions
- An account or proposal you shaped, how you shaped it, and how its teams delivered afterwards

### Self-check

- Do senior stakeholders seek my judgment, or only my expertise and my team's output?
- When client deadlines and the teams' pace pull apart, which one do my decisions protect?
- Will the architecture and direction I shaped still be right in several years?

### Not yet

- The client relationship depends on you personally rather than on how the account is run
- Decisions that sound right but don't survive real trade-offs
- The account grows, or proposals win new work, while the teams burn out or their delivery slips

## People & Team

> I improve how people grow beyond my own team.

### What changes

At Team you grow the people on your team; at Wider you grow **mentors and leaders**, and how people grow across teams.

### Expectations

#### Growing others

- **Develop mentors and leads**: I **develop other mentors, coaches and leads**, so people I never work with directly grow as well
  - Coach **the people who mentor, coach or lead their teams**
  - **People I never work with directly** grow because of the mentors and leads I developed
  - Mentor and coach **people across Sahaj**, not only on my own team or account
- **Sponsor people across teams**: I create growth opportunities for people **beyond my own team**, and sponsor people into them
  - Create opportunities, such as a new role or a stretch assignment, for people **beyond my own team**
  - **Put people forward** for them, and back them while they grow into them

#### Feedback and trust

- **Improve how people grow across teams**: I improve how people **across teams** learn, give feedback and grow, through practices other people carry on
  - Improve a practice for learning, feedback or growth that **several teams** use
  - **Other people carry the practice on** without me

#### Communication and collaboration

- **Align several teams**: I communicate direction so **several teams align** to it, including in high-stakes conversations
  - Explain direction so that **several teams** understand it and act on it
  - Handle **high-stakes conversations**, such as with a client's leadership, calmly and clearly

### Evidence

- People you sponsored into new responsibilities or developed as mentors and leads, where they are now, and the people they now develop
- A practice or forum for growing people across teams that you set up and handed to people who now run it
- Direction you set that several teams understood the same way and acted on

### Self-check

- Am I building capability that will outlast me, or only growing the people I work with?

### Not yet

- Mentors the same few people they would have mentored anyway
- Opportunities go to people already visible, rather than to people who are ready

## Org & Community

> I shape how Sahaj grows, how it works, or how it is seen.

### What changes

At Team you build programmes and tools that other people keep running; at Wider you shape **how Sahaj grows, works or is seen**.

### Expectations

- **Design what Sahaj needs**: I see what Sahaj needs and **shape the response**, designing initiatives rather than only running them
  - Spot **what Sahaj needs** before anyone asks for it
  - **Design the initiative** that answers it, and bring other people in to run it
- **Make a lasting change**: I make **a lasting change** to how Sahaj builds capability, looks after its culture, is seen or wins work, and the change stays in place after I step back
  - Change **how Sahaj** builds capability, looks after its culture, is seen or wins work
  - The change **stays in place** after I step back
  - Where my reach is the wider community, change how **engineers outside Sahaj** work, and become someone they learn from

### Evidence

- Initiatives you designed, and their effect beyond your own office or account
- Changes to how Sahaj works that are still in use
- External work that led to adoption, changed practice, stronger capability or new opportunities for Sahaj

### Self-check

- Does what I designed carry on beyond my own office or account, or only where I keep running it myself?

### Not yet

- Recognised outside Sahaj far more than relied on inside it
- Ideas that stay as ideas, without the effort to win over the people needed to make them happen
- Innovating alone, without bringing along the people the change affects

### Ways to contribute

#### Culture and organisational health

- Fixing an organisation-level cultural problem with practices that people keep to under delivery or commercial pressure

#### Initiatives and capability

- Shaping how Sahaj hires or onboards, beyond one office
- Changing how Sahaj hires and grows engineers as AI changes the work, such as interview problems that assess how a candidate frames a problem and checks a result before they write any code
- Shaping proposals or pitches for new clients or other accounts, from framing the problem to the approach
- Taking what you learned in Emerge into how Sahaj works, or shaping how the programme runs

#### Community and external contribution

- Leading Sahaj's thinking in a domain, inside and outside, in a way that changes how teams work or brings in work
- Leading Sahaj's thinking on AI-assisted engineering, so teams across Sahaj and at its clients change how they deliver
- Speaking at major industry conferences or publishing writing that changes how engineers in the industry work, or brings people or work to Sahaj
- Shaping Sahaj's flagship DevDay so it changes how Sahaj is seen and brings in people, clients or work

## Ways to create impact

There is more than one way to have impact at this scope, and most people combine several. The expectations above describe the impact, not the role: leading an account is one way, not a requirement. Whichever you take, you need impact in all three areas.

### Leading an account

You shape Sahaj's relationship with a client by building it and growing the account, whether it has one team or several: the roadmap, proposals and the demand pipeline.

### Technical direction

You set the direction the account, or teams across Sahaj, follow, and you protect Sahaj's technical reputation. Other teams using a tool or practice you built isn't enough on its own: the impact is Wider when the tool or practice shapes how those teams decide and work, and lasts beyond your involvement.

### Capability and culture

You design how Sahaj builds capability, hires, onboards and looks after its culture.

### External thought leadership

You shape how the industry thinks through writing, talks and open source. Visibility alone isn't impact: what counts is what the writing, talks or code led to, such as reputation that brings work to Sahaj.

## Examples

### Meets expectations

You grew an account from one team to three, kept delivery predictable and the pace sustainable as it grew, and set the architecture and delivery approach all three follow. Senior client stakeholders bring you their strategic questions, and the roadmap they are now executing is one you shaped. You sponsored two engineers into leading teams on the account, and inside Sahaj you designed a capability track on the account's domain that other offices now use.

Without leading an account: the event-driven architecture you shaped is the approach three of the client's teams now follow, and the client's architecture group asks for your view on decisions you aren't part of. You grew two engineers into the people those teams turn to on it, and they now coach people on those teams. Inside Sahaj you designed how offices build capability in that domain, and people in those offices now run it.

### Below expectations

You kept the account's immediate commitments, but didn't set direction or build capability its teams could sustain without you. The direction you set lived mostly in conversations and decks, teams interpreted it differently, and under delivery pressure it was dropped without anyone deciding to drop it.
