## Summary

A flat organisation runs on people choosing to build it. This area covers what you contribute beyond your immediate delivery: culture, initiatives, capability and the wider community.

## Ways to contribute

Unlike the facets in the other two areas, nobody is expected to show all three.

### Culture and organisational health

Upholding how we work, speaking up when a practice or decision doesn't fit it, and fixing what wears people down.

### Initiatives and capability

Contributing to, driving and eventually designing the programmes and initiatives that make Sahaj better, such as capability development, hiring, onboarding and internal tools.

### Community and external contribution

Giving back inside and outside Sahaj through sessions, events such as DevDay, writing, talks and open source.

## Contributing is expected of everyone

<!-- band -->
*What you contribute is your choice. Whether you contribute is not.*

There is no required blog, talk or number of initiatives, and the natural way to contribute is through what you're good at and what motivates you: a specialist often contributes through their speciality, such as a capability track, a talk or a shared tool. The expectation is a contribution you work on steadily through the cycle, with a result your teammates can see, made in working time. Your team makes room for it, so it doesn't spill into your evenings: someone on the team agrees with you which work moves to make that time. Sahaj is flat: there is no separate team building culture and capability on everyone else's behalf, so if some people opt out, the people who stay in carry the load.

## Examples at each scope

*Each scope has its own examples under the same three ways to contribute.*

Each scope has its own examples under the same three ways to contribute, and they show the kind of contribution expected there. Anything else counts too, judged by what it changed.

## What counts

*What counts is meaningful impact, or a genuine attempt to create it, not just taking part in an activity.*

A quiet fix that removes recurring frustration can be more valuable than a highly visible event.

Not every attempt will succeed, and that's fine when you took it on properly and stayed with it; say what you tried and what it left behind.

The impact expected grows with your scope. Organising a talk is a good contribution at Direct, but not at Wider. At Extended, drive work beyond your project. At Team, build a programme, forum or tool that other people keep running. At Wider, bring about lasting change across Sahaj.

## How it is assessed

*No sustained contribution beyond your delivery this cycle means Building Sahaj is below expectations, whatever your other areas look like, unless the seniors on your team, or your account lead, agreed there was no room.*

If your project leaves no room, agree it with the seniors on your team, or with your account lead if the cause is account-wide, such as a new account being set up or a delivery crunch. If you know at the start of the cycle, record it in your doc when you agree your expectations; if it comes up later, raise it as soon as you know. Either way, agree what contribution is still possible, who will make time for it, and a date to look at the arrangement again. Raised for the first time in your self-assessment, it's too late to change anything.

You're then rated on what was reasonably available to you, and the constraint is recorded in your doc. If an agreed deferral covered the whole cycle, Building Sahaj is recorded as not rated for that cycle, with the reason, and it doesn't count toward your scope growing.

Like every area, Building Sahaj counts when your scope grows: you need consistent next-scope impact here as well as in the other two.
