## Summary

A flat organisation runs on people choosing to build it. This area covers what you contribute beyond your immediate delivery: culture, initiatives, capability and the wider community.

## Ways to contribute

Unlike the facets in the other two areas, nobody is expected to show all three.

### Culture and organisational health

Upholding how we work, speaking up when a practice or decision doesn't fit it, and fixing what wears people down.

### Initiatives and capability

Contributing to, driving and eventually designing the programmes and initiatives that make Sahaj better, such as capability development, hiring, onboarding and internal tools.

### Community and external contribution

Giving back inside and outside Sahaj through sessions, events such as DevDay, writing, talks and open source.

## Contributing is expected of everyone

<!-- band -->
*What you contribute is your choice. Whether you contribute is not.*

There is no required blog, talk or number of initiatives, and the natural way to contribute is through what you're good at and what motivates you: a specialist often contributes through their speciality, such as a capability track, a talk or a shared tool. The expectation is a contribution you keep up through the cycle and can point to, made in working time. Your team makes room for it, so it doesn't spill into your evenings: someone on the team agrees with you which work moves to make that time. Sahaj is flat: there is no separate team building culture and capability on everyone else's behalf, so if some people opt out, the people who stay in carry the load.

## Examples at each scope

*Each scope has its own examples under the same three ways to contribute.*

Each scope has its own examples under the same three ways to contribute, and they show the kind of contribution expected there. Anything else counts too, judged by what it changed.

## What counts

*Contribution is judged on what changed, not on how visible it was, and it has to be sustained through the cycle.*

A low-profile fix that removes recurring friction for many people can be worth more than a highly visible contribution. An initiative that didn't succeed still counts when you took it on properly, stayed with it, and were stopped by a cause outside your control; say what you tried and what it left behind. One attempt dropped at the first obstacle does not count. The expected reach grows with your scope: contributing at Direct, driving a piece of org work at Extended, building a programme, forum or tool that other people keep running at Team, and shaping Sahaj's capability, culture or reputation at Wider. Anyone can contribute beyond that, and it counts.

## How it is assessed

*No sustained contribution beyond your delivery this cycle means Org & Community is below expectations, whatever your other areas look like, unless your team agreed there was no room.*

If your project leaves no room, agree it with the seniors on your team, or with your account lead if the cause is account-wide, such as a new account being set up or a delivery crunch. If you know at the start of the cycle, record it in your doc when you agree your expectations; if it comes up later, raise it as soon as you know. Either way, agree what contribution is still possible, who will make time for it, and a date to look at the arrangement again. Raised for the first time in your self-assessment, it's too late to change anything.

You're then rated on what was reasonably available to you, and the constraint is recorded in your doc. If an agreed deferral covered the whole cycle, Org & Community is recorded as not rated for that cycle, with the reason, and it doesn't count toward a raise.

Like every area, Org & Community counts toward a raise in scope: you need consistent next-scope impact here as well as in the other two.
