## Summary

This is the work we are paid for: building the right thing, building it well and delivering it reliably. It covers technical craft, consulting and delivery together, because on a client engagement they depend on each other.

## Facets

### Technical craft

Clean, tested, maintainable engineering, from code and design to architecture, security and how your work runs in production. It is the baseline at every scope, but craft alone won't get you to the next one.

### Consulting

Understanding the problem, removing ambiguity, proposing options and helping stakeholders decide. Doing what is asked without questioning an unclear or risky ask is not consulting.

### Delivery

Predictable, sustainable delivery: planning, visible progress, early risks and trade-offs made explicit. Late-night heroics and constant firefighting are signs that delivery needs fixing.

## How facets are rated

Merging three capabilities into one area makes the guide easier to hold in your head, but it also makes it easier to hide a gap, so every scope lists its expectations by facet. All three facets count toward one rating: strong technical craft cannot average out weak consulting. When you set expectations or assess against this area, name each facet, and treat a clear gap in one of them, meaning its impact isn't shown consistently at your scope, as making the area below expectations.

Consulting deserves particular care, because it is where self-assessment is least reliable. Almost everyone believes they consult well, so ask what changed because of how you framed a problem; the meetings you attended don't show that.
