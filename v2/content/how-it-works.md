## Overview

Everyone has one scope, which describes how far their impact is expected to reach. The scope sets what is expected of you in three areas of work, and you grow by taking on some of the next scope's responsibilities until they become consistent.

## Scopes

- **Direct**: your own work
- **Extended**: work beyond your own
- **Team**: the whole team
- **Wider**: beyond the team

A wider scope still includes the work of the ones inside it.

## Areas

- **Client & Delivery**: building the right thing, building it well and delivering it reliably.
- **People & Team**: helping the people around you become more effective.
- **Org & Community**: contributing to Sahaj beyond your immediate delivery. You choose how, but contributing is part of the job.

Learning runs through all three. At every scope you keep your craft and judgment current, act on feedback, and adapt when the context changes.

## Your reference scope

Your reference scope is the scope you have shown consistently across all three areas. You choose it when you write your expectations. If it doesn't match the impact your reviewers have seen, it is corrected during the review, before the doc is agreed. It sets the same expectations in every area.

You will be stronger in some areas than others, and the rating captures that: below, meets or exceeds expectations in each area. Exceeds means impact clearly stronger, broader or more durable than your scope expects; occasional next-scope work is a stretch rather than exceeding. Meeting your scope in one area doesn't make up for falling short in another.

## Growing into the next scope

- **Meet your scope**: consistently, in all three areas
- **Take on a stretch**: some of the next scope's responsibilities
- **Others rely on it**: the stretch becomes consistent
- **Your scope is raised**: at the team check

A stretch usually starts in one area and adds to your current expectations rather than replacing them. Nobody expects you to perform fully at the next scope before it is recognised.

## The cycle

- **Write your expectations**: choose your scope and any stretch, and draft your doc
- **Get it reviewed**: teammates ask for changes until it's agreed
- **Collect feedback**: feedback goes into the same doc, throughout the cycle
- **Self-assess**: write it in the doc, then discuss it at the team check

You drive your own doc. Senior teammates usually review it for others on the team, and a senior's doc is reviewed by seniors on other projects in the account or the office; [Reviewing and feedback](#teammates) is the guide for reviewers. Because feedback builds up in the doc through the cycle, your self-assessment starts from evidence already written down, and at the team check you can present the doc or let others read it for context.

## Principles

- **Expectations are written down first.** If they were never made explicit, that is the first gap to fix.
- **Evidence is a pattern.** One good example shows you can; consistency, and others relying on it, shows you do.
- **Roles are routes to impact.** No scope requires a particular role, and leading a team or an account doesn't set your scope.
- **Growth means wider trust.** Doing more of the same work, or doing it better, is not the next scope.
- **You can always go beyond your scope.** Your scope sets what's expected, never what you're allowed to do.
- **Every office uses the same guide.** "Team scope" means the same thing in Pune as in London.
