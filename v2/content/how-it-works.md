## Scopes

<!-- group: Scopes | How far your impact is expected to reach | scope -->
- **Direct**: your own work
- **Extended**: work beyond your own
- **Team**: the whole team
- **Wider**: beyond the team

A wider scope still includes the ones inside it: at Team, your own work and your help with other people's work still count.

## Scopes in practice

One piece of client work, handled at each scope. The example is made up, and it follows Client & Delivery only; every scope expects impact in all three areas.

## Your scope

*Your scope is the one you have shown consistently across all three areas, and it sets what's expected of you in each.*

Your scope is the one you have shown consistently across all three areas. You choose it when you write your expectations. If it doesn't match the impact your teammates have seen, it is corrected during the review, before the doc is agreed. It sets the same expectations in every area. If you're new to Sahaj, it is agreed from the impact you showed in your previous work, and nobody expects you to meet every expectation in your first few weeks, while you learn what's new to you.

You will be stronger in some areas than others, and the rating captures that: below, meets or exceeds expectations in each area. Exceeds means impact clearly stronger, broader or more durable than your scope expects.

<!-- callout -->
Exceeds is judged against your current scope, and it doesn't change your scope: that happens only when your next-scope impact is consistent in all three areas. Being strong in one area doesn't make up for falling short in another.

## Areas

<!-- group: Areas | Where your impact counts, each with its own rating | areas -->
*Every scope covers the same three areas of impact, each with its own rating.*

- **Client & Delivery**: building what the client needs, building it well and owning what the team commits to deliver.
- **People & Team**: helping the people around you become more effective.
- **Org & Community**: contributing to Sahaj beyond your immediate delivery. You choose how, but contributing is part of the job.

Client & Delivery and People & Team each have three [facets](#area/client-delivery), and all three count toward the area's one rating. The lines under a facet show what it looks like at your scope; they are signs to look for, and nobody ticks them off one by one. Org & Community has [ways to contribute](#area/org-community) instead: expectations shared by everyone at your scope, and ways to contribute that you choose from to meet them.

<!-- callout -->
Each area gets one rating, judged across its facets as a whole rather than counted. Exceeding your scope's expectations in two facets can carry a weaker third, but a facet you don't show at your scope at all makes the area below expectations.

All three areas are part of everyone's job, and within each you choose how you contribute: someone who doesn't enjoy writing can give a talk or build a tool instead. What you can't choose is to leave an area, or a facet, to other people.

Learning has no area of its own because it runs through all three: at every scope you keep your craft and judgment current, act on feedback, and adapt when the context changes.

## Specialist agreements

*At Extended scope and above, a strength your team already relies on can let you hand one responsibility to a teammate and contribute more through that strength instead.*

Some people do their best work by going deep. A specialist agreement lets you do that without leaving gaps for the people around you: you hand one specific responsibility to a teammate, such as leading the client's planning conversations, and agree a stronger contribution through your strength. It hands over a responsibility within a facet, never a whole facet or area, so you still show every facet through the parts you keep.

An agreement needs three things, written in your expectations doc and agreed with your team:

- **A strength the team already relies on**, in technical craft, consulting or delivery ownership, shown in your past cycles. Interest in a subject or years of experience aren't enough on their own.
- **A teammate who accepts the responsibility.** Name them in your doc; the work counts as their impact, and they take it on by choice, not by default.
- **A stronger contribution from you**: what you will achieve through your strength, who it helps and how they will use it, starting from the next scope's expectations for that strength. At Wider, where there is no next scope, agree the outcome directly.

Some expectations stay with everyone, whatever they hand over: communicating progress and risks, treating people with respect, giving and acting on feedback, keeping your commitments, and doing your part of anything you handed over, such as giving the estimates and risks a client conversation depends on. Agreements start at Extended because Direct is where you learn every part of the work, and a gap left there follows you into every later scope. Review the agreement at the team check, or sooner if the team or its work changes. How depth is recognised, and how it grows your scope, is on [Specialisation](#specialisation).

## The cycle

<!-- group: The cycle | Set expectations, gather feedback, look back | cycle -->
- **Write your expectations**: choose your scope and any stretch, and draft your doc
- **Get it reviewed**: teammates ask for changes until it's agreed
- **Gather feedback**: ask for it often, keep notes, and check all three areas halfway through
- **Self-assess**: write it in the doc, then discuss it at the team check

You drive your own doc, a short one made from [the template](doc-templates/expectations.docx): your scope, anything specific to your work, any specialist agreement or agreed deferral, where you want to grow, and later your self-assessment. The expectations themselves stay in this guide, so the doc only notes your scope. Upload it to Google Drive to work on it as a Google Doc and share it with your team. [Reviewing and feedback](#teammates) is the guide for the people who review it. Through the cycle you ask the people you work with for feedback, often and while the work is fresh, and keep notes of what they tell you, wherever you keep notes. Halfway through the cycle, you go through all three areas with the teammates who reviewed your doc, in a [mid-cycle check](#feedback/the-mid-cycle-check), so nothing that is expected of you comes up for the first time at the end. That way your [self-assessment](#self-assessment) starts from what you wrote down at the time. The cycle ends with the team check, a conversation in which teammates from your project and office confirm, correct or add to your self-assessment; you can present the doc there, or let your teammates read it beforehand for context. A raise in scope is decided there too, and the next cycle's doc is written at the new scope.

## Who reviews and decides

*Anyone on your team can review your expectations and confirm your ratings, whatever their scope.*

Anyone on your team can review your expectations, discuss them and ask for changes, whatever their scope, and at the team check your teammates confirm, correct or add to your self-assessment and confirm your ratings. Review goes in every direction: the people a senior's work reaches, such as the teammates they help grow, often see that impact most clearly.

If you and your teammates still disagree after discussing it, about your scope or a rating, the team's seniors make a judgement call, leaving out the person whose doc it is. If that doesn't settle it, the account lead decides, or, for an account lead's own doc, a founder or the region's managing director.

## Growing into the next scope

<!-- group: Growth | Take on some of the next scope's responsibilities | growth -->
- **Meet your scope**: consistently, in all three areas
- **Take on more**: some of the next scope's responsibilities, usually as a stretch
- **Keep it up**: until the people around you depend on you for it, in all three areas
- **Your scope is raised**: at the team check

A stretch adds some of the next scope's responsibilities on top of your current expectations. It usually starts in one area, and consistent next-scope impact there is rated as exceeding. Your scope is raised once you show that impact consistently in all three areas, because your scope is the one you show everywhere. It doesn't need every next-scope expectation met perfectly, only a pattern the people around you depend on; a single strong instance isn't enough, and there is no fixed number of cycles.

A stretch is the usual way to grow, but you don't need one on record to be raised: impact that is already consistent at the next scope counts however it started. If you are growing quickly, you can take on next-scope responsibilities as soon as you're ready, in one area or more. If you have been at the same scope for a long time, or your scope is below what your experience suggests, a stretch is set with you in all three areas, so that your growth has a plan; a specialist can make it through their strength in each area.

Next-scope impact can come through a strength. A specialist whose expertise the whole team now works in, without needing them for it, is showing Team scope in People & Team as well as in Client & Delivery. What counts is your own impact: work a teammate took on under a specialist agreement counts toward their growth, not yours.
