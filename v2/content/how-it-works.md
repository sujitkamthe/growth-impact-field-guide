## Scopes

<!-- group: Scopes | How far your impact is expected to reach | scope -->
- **Direct**: your own work
- **Extended**: work beyond your own
- **Team**: the whole team
- **Wider**: beyond the team

A wider scope still includes the ones inside it: at Team, your own work and your help with other people's work still count.

## Scopes in practice

One piece of client work, handled at each scope. The example is made up, and it follows Client & Delivery only; every scope expects impact in all three areas.

## Your scope

*Your scope is the one you have shown consistently across all three areas, and it sets what's expected of you in each.*

Your scope is the one you have shown consistently across all three areas, and you choose it when you write your expectations. If your teammates have seen different impact, they say so while they read your doc, and the scope is corrected before the doc is agreed. If you're new to Sahaj, your team agrees your first scope with you from the impact you had in your previous work.

You will be stronger in some areas than others, and each area's rating, below, meets or exceeds expectations, shows that. [Self-assessment](#self-assessment/choosing-a-rating) says what each rating means.

<!-- callout -->
Exceeds is judged against your current scope, and it doesn't change your scope: your scope grows only when your contribution consistently reaches the next scope in all three areas. Being strong in one area doesn't make up for falling short in another.

## Areas

<!-- group: Areas | Where your impact counts, each with its own rating | areas -->
*Every scope covers the same three areas of impact, each with its own rating.*

- **Client & Delivery**: building what the client needs, building it well and owning what the team commits to deliver.
- **People & Team**: helping the people around you become more effective.
- **Org & Community**: contributing to Sahaj beyond your immediate delivery. You choose how, but contributing is expected of everyone.

Client & Delivery and People & Team each have three [facets](#area/client-delivery), and all three count toward the area's one rating. The lines under a facet show what it looks like at your scope; they are signs to look for, and nobody ticks them off one by one. Org & Community has [ways to contribute](#area/org-community) instead: expectations shared by everyone at your scope, and ways to contribute that you choose from to meet them.

<!-- callout -->
Each area gets one rating, judged across its facets as a whole. Your strengths can be uneven, but a facet you don't show at your scope makes the area below expectations.

All three areas are part of what's expected of everyone, and within each you choose how you contribute: someone who doesn't enjoy writing can give a talk or build a tool instead. What you can't do is leave an area to other people. From Extended scope, you can agree with your team to take less of a particular responsibility and contribute more through a strength, which is called an [agreed focus](#specialisation); most people don't need one.

Learning has no area of its own because it runs through all three: at every scope you keep your craft and judgment current, act on feedback, and adapt when the context changes.

## The cycle

<!-- group: The cycle | Set expectations, gather feedback, look back | cycle -->
- **Write your expectations**: confirm your scope and write your doc for the cycle
- **Get it agreed**: teammates who know your work read it and ask for changes
- **Gather feedback**: ask for it often, and keep notes
- **Self-assess**: write it in the doc, then discuss it at the team check

[Agree your expectations](#agree) walks you through the doc, with a finished example. Through the cycle, ask the people you work with for feedback while the work is fresh, and keep notes of what they tell you, so your [self-assessment](#self-assessment) starts from what you wrote down at the time; [Feedback](#feedback) explains how to give and ask for it.

The cycle ends with the team check, a conversation in which teammates from your project and office confirm, correct or add to your self-assessment, confirm your ratings and decide whether your scope has grown. Each team check also looks ahead: where evidence is missing, and what changes next cycle, so an expectation comes up in the cycle it applies to rather than later.

## Who reviews and decides

*Anyone on your team can read your expectations and confirm your ratings, whatever their scope, and you ask the teammates who know your work.*

Anyone on your team can read your expectations, discuss them and ask for changes, whatever their scope. You ask the teammates who know your work across the three areas, and the doc is agreed when you and they can both point to what's expected. At the team check, your teammates confirm, correct or add to your self-assessment and confirm your ratings. Review goes in every direction: the people a senior's work reaches, such as the teammates they help grow, often see that impact most clearly.

If you and your teammates still disagree after discussing it, about your scope or a rating, the team's seniors make a judgement call, leaving out the person whose doc it is. If that doesn't settle it, the account lead decides, or, for an account lead's own doc, a founder or the region's managing director.

## Growing into the next scope

<!-- group: Growth | Deepen what you do, or reach further | growth -->
- **Meet your scope**: consistently, in all three areas
- **Take on more**: some of the next scope's responsibilities, or a greater contribution through your strength
- **Keep it up**: until the people around you depend on you for it
- **Your scope grows**: at the team check

Growing doesn't only mean moving to the next scope. You can grow by deepening your expertise, sharpening your judgment, helping the people around you become more effective or extending your impact, and all of it counts in your assessment. Your scope grows when that growth reaches further: when your contribution consistently reaches the next scope in all three areas, with the collaboration and judgment it needs at that reach. With an [agreed focus](#specialisation), each area is judged against it, so your People & Team impact at the next scope can come through your strength, such as a team that now works in your domain without needing you. What counts is your own contribution; a teammate's work never counts toward your scope.

A stretch adds some of the next scope's responsibilities on top of your current expectations. It usually starts in one area, and consistent next-scope impact there counts toward Exceeds. Your scope doesn't need every next-scope expectation met perfectly to grow, only a pattern the people around you depend on; a single strong instance isn't enough, and there is no fixed number of cycles.

A stretch is the usual way to reach further, but you don't need one on record: impact that is already consistent at the next scope counts however it started. If you are growing quickly, you can take on next-scope responsibilities as soon as you're ready. If you have been at the same scope for a long time, or your scope is below what your experience suggests, talk with your team about whether your work still challenges you, and agree a development goal with the opportunities and support it needs. The goal can deepen your contribution at your current scope or prepare you for the next one.
