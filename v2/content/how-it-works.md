## The short version

Everyone has one scope, which describes how far their impact is expected to reach. The scope sets what is expected of you in three areas of work. You grow by sustaining impact at your scope across all three areas, then taking on some of the next scope's responsibilities until they become consistent too.

## Four scopes

- **Direct**: impact through work directly in your hands, such as the stories and features you own end to end.
- **Extended**: impact that improves work beyond your own, such as features other people own, a stream you lead or a teammate you help.
- **Team**: impact that changes how the whole team operates, performs and grows.
- **Wider**: impact that crosses the team boundary, into a multi-team account, the client's organisation, Sahaj or the wider community.

Each scope includes the ones inside it. A scope describes the responsibility expected of you, not a title: "my scope is Team" is fine, "I'm a Team" isn't.

## Three areas

- **Client & Delivery**: building the right thing, building it well and delivering it reliably.
- **People & Team**: helping the people around you become more effective.
- **Org & Community**: contributing to Sahaj beyond your immediate delivery. You choose how; whether is not optional.

## One scope per person

Your **reference scope** is the scope you have shown consistently across all three areas, as confirmed at your last team check. It sets the same expectation in every area.

You will be stronger in some areas than others, and that is normal. How strongly you meet your scope in each area is what the rating captures: below, meets or exceeds expectations. Meeting it in one area does not make up for falling well short in another.

## Growing into the next scope

Once your scope is established, you start taking on some of the next scope's responsibilities, usually in one area first. That is a **stretch**. It adds to your current expectations rather than replacing them, and you reflect on it separately in your self-assessment. When the stretch has become consistent and others rely on it, the team check raises your reference scope.

Nobody expects you to perform fully at the next scope before it is recognised.

## How it relates to pay

Your reference scope is the anchor for compensation, with one scope and one band. A stretch describes where you are growing and does not by itself change pay. Two people at the same scope can have different strengths, but both are expected to create impact at that scope in all three areas.

## A cycle

1. **Set expectations.** At the start of the cycle, you and your teammates confirm your reference scope, agree any stretch, and write it down on an expectation sheet.
2. **Work and give feedback.** Feedback happens throughout the cycle against the sheet, not saved for the end.
3. **Self-assess.** You bring evidence of what changed because of you, and show how you have understood your feedback.
4. **Team check.** Teammates confirm, correct or add to your assessment, and you do the same for them. Then you set the next cycle's expectations.

## Principles

### Expectations are written before they are assessed

If they were never made explicit, that is the first gap to fix.

### Evidence is a pattern

A single good instance shows you can. Consistency across the cycle, and others relying on it, shows you do.

### Scope, not quantity

Doing more of the same work, or doing it better, is not growth to the next scope. Growth means what you are trusted with gets wider.

### A scope is a floor, not a ceiling

It sets what is expected of you, never what you are allowed to do. Anyone can contribute beyond their scope, and it counts.

### One guide, every office

Every office uses the same scopes, areas and sheet, so "Team scope" means the same thing in Pune as in London.

## Words we use

- **Scope**: how far your impact is expected to reach. There are four.
- **Area**: one of the three kinds of work every scope covers.
- **Reference scope**: your confirmed scope, which sets your expectations and your pay band.
- **Stretch**: next-scope responsibilities you are taking on this cycle, in addition to your current ones.
- **Expectation sheet**: the written record of what was agreed for a cycle, shared as a link.
- **Team check**: the conversation where teammates confirm, correct or add to each other's self-assessments.
