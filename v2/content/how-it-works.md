## Scopes

<!-- group: Scopes | How far your impact is expected to reach | scope -->
- **Direct**: your own work
- **Extended**: work beyond your own
- **Team**: the whole team
- **Wider**: beyond the team

A wider scope still includes the ones inside it: at Team, your own work and your help with other people's work still count.

## Scopes in practice

One piece of client work, handled at each scope. The example is made up, and it follows Client & Delivery only; every scope expects impact in all three areas.

## Your scope

*Your scope is the one you have shown consistently across all three areas, and it sets what's expected of you in each.*

Your scope is the one you have shown consistently across all three areas. You choose it when you write your expectations. If it doesn't match the impact your teammates have seen, it is corrected during the review, before the doc is agreed. It sets the same expectations in every area; if you have an [agreed focus](#how-it-works/agreeing-a-focus), each area is judged against it. If you're new to Sahaj, it is agreed from the impact you showed in your previous work, and nobody expects you to meet every expectation in your first few weeks, while you learn what's new to you.

You will be stronger in some areas than others, and the rating captures that: below, meets or exceeds expectations in each area. Exceeds means impact clearly stronger, broader or more durable than your scope expects.

<!-- callout -->
Exceeds is judged against your current scope, and it doesn't change your scope: your scope grows only when your contribution consistently reaches the next scope in all three areas. Being strong in one area doesn't make up for falling short in another.

## Areas

<!-- group: Areas | Where your impact counts, each with its own rating | areas -->
*Every scope covers the same three areas of impact, each with its own rating.*

- **Client & Delivery**: building what the client needs, building it well and owning what the team commits to deliver.
- **People & Team**: helping the people around you become more effective.
- **Org & Community**: contributing to Sahaj beyond your immediate delivery. You choose how, but contributing is expected of everyone.

Client & Delivery and People & Team each have three [facets](#area/client-delivery), and all three count toward the area's one rating. The lines under a facet show what it looks like at your scope; they are signs to look for, and nobody ticks them off one by one. Org & Community has [ways to contribute](#area/org-community) instead: expectations shared by everyone at your scope, and ways to contribute that you choose from to meet them.

<!-- callout -->
Each area gets one rating, judged across its facets as a whole rather than counted. Exceeding your scope's expectations in two facets can carry a weaker third, but a facet you don't show at your scope at all makes the area below expectations. With an agreed focus, a facet is judged against the responsibilities you kept in it.

All three areas are part of what's expected of everyone, and within each you choose how you contribute: someone who doesn't enjoy writing can give a talk or build a tool instead. What you can't do is leave an area to other people; taking less of a particular responsibility needs an agreed focus.

Learning has no area of its own because it runs through all three: at every scope you keep your craft and judgment current, act on feedback, and adapt when the context changes.

## Agreeing a focus

*At Extended scope and above, you can agree with your team to make a strength your main contribution, take less of some other responsibilities, and contribute more through that strength in return.*

Everyone grows through their strengths, and anyone can choose one to develop without agreeing anything. An agreed focus goes further: you take less of a particular responsibility, such as leading the client's planning conversations or formally mentoring teammates, so you can contribute more where you are strongest. It changes the shape of what's expected of you, not the standard: you stay fully accountable for everything you keep.

You propose it in your expectations doc, and your team agrees it:

- **Where you'll contribute most**, and the results you've already shown there, beyond what your scope normally expects. Interest or years of experience aren't enough on their own; if you're still building that expertise, agree a trial for one cycle, with support.
- **The responsibilities you'll take less of**: specific ones, from the right-hand column below, never a whole area.
- **The greater contribution you'll make in return**: the result, who it helps and how your teammates will recognise it, starting from the next scope's expectations for that strength. At Wider, where there is no next scope, agree the result directly.
- **Who takes on what you hand over**: a named teammate who chooses to take it on and has room for it. Agree what work moves for both of you; their part counts toward their own growth.

Your team agrees a focus when the greater contribution meets a real need and the handover is sustainable for the teammate taking it on.

| | What stays with everyone | What you can take less of |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical craft** | Work that meets the team's engineering standards, and understanding and explaining its technical risks | Technical ownership beyond your own work, such as leading the architecture across the engagement |
| **Consulting** | Understanding the client's problem, questioning unclear or risky requests, and explaining the options in your own work | Leading discovery, workshops or stakeholder negotiations |
| **Delivery ownership** | Realistic commitments for your own work, visible progress and risks, and following through when plans change | Coordinating the team's delivery, managing dependencies, or leading planning conversations with the client |
| **People & Team** | Working respectfully, sharing context, and giving and acting on feedback | Formal mentoring, coaching several people, or organising how the team develops |
| **Org & Community** | A contribution beyond your project, in a way you choose | Nothing: contributing stays expected of everyone |

An agreed focus starts at Extended because Direct is where you learn every part of the work, and a gap left there follows you into every later scope. Look at it at each team check: whether the greater contribution is happening, and whether the work is still sustainable for both of you. How depth and breadth are recognised is on [Specialisation](#specialisation).

## The cycle

<!-- group: The cycle | Set expectations, gather feedback, look back | cycle -->
- **Write your expectations**: choose your scope and any stretch, and draft your doc
- **Get it reviewed**: teammates ask for changes until it's agreed
- **Gather feedback**: ask for it often, and keep notes
- **Self-assess**: write it in the doc, then discuss it at the team check

You drive your own doc, a short one made from [the template](doc-templates/expectations.docx): your scope, anything specific to your work, any agreed focus or agreed deferral, where you want to grow, and later your self-assessment. The expectations themselves stay in this guide, so the doc only notes your scope. Upload it to Google Drive to work on it as a Google Doc and share it with your team. [Reviewing and feedback](#teammates) is the guide for the people who review it. Through the cycle you ask the people you work with for feedback, often and while the work is fresh, and keep notes of what they tell you, wherever you keep notes. That way your [self-assessment](#self-assessment) starts from what you wrote down at the time. The cycle ends with the team check, a conversation in which teammates from your project and office confirm, correct or add to your self-assessment; you can present the doc there, or let your teammates read it beforehand for context. Whether your scope has grown is decided there too. Each team check also looks ahead: where evidence is missing, especially in an area or facet nobody has commented on, and what changes next cycle, so an expectation comes up in the cycle it applies to, not later. The next cycle's doc is written at your scope then.

## Who reviews and decides

*Anyone on your team can review your expectations and confirm your ratings, whatever their scope.*

Anyone on your team can review your expectations, discuss them and ask for changes, whatever their scope, and at the team check your teammates confirm, correct or add to your self-assessment and confirm your ratings. Review goes in every direction: the people a senior's work reaches, such as the teammates they help grow, often see that impact most clearly.

If you and your teammates still disagree after discussing it, about your scope or a rating, the team's seniors make a judgement call, leaving out the person whose doc it is. If that doesn't settle it, the account lead decides, or, for an account lead's own doc, a founder or the region's managing director.

## Growing into the next scope

<!-- group: Growth | Deepen what you do, or reach further | growth -->
- **Meet your scope**: consistently, in all three areas
- **Take on more**: some of the next scope's responsibilities, or a greater contribution through your strength
- **Keep it up**: until the people around you depend on you for it
- **Your scope grows**: at the team check

Growing doesn't only mean moving to the next scope. You can grow by deepening your expertise, sharpening your judgment, helping the people around you become more effective or extending your impact, and all of it counts in your assessment. Your scope grows when that growth reaches further: when your contribution consistently reaches the next scope in all three areas, with the collaboration and judgment it needs at that reach. With an agreed focus, each area is judged against it, so your People & Team impact at the next scope can come through your strength, such as a team that now works in your domain without needing you. What counts is your own contribution; a teammate's work never counts toward your scope.

A stretch adds some of the next scope's responsibilities on top of your current expectations. It usually starts in one area, and consistent next-scope impact there is rated as exceeding. Your scope doesn't need every next-scope expectation met perfectly to grow, only a pattern the people around you depend on; a single strong instance isn't enough, and there is no fixed number of cycles.

A stretch is the usual way to reach further, but you don't need one on record: impact that is already consistent at the next scope counts however it started. If you are growing quickly, you can take on next-scope responsibilities as soon as you're ready. If you have been at the same scope for a long time, or your scope is below what your experience suggests, talk with your team about whether your work still challenges you, and agree a development goal with the opportunities and support it needs. The goal can deepen your contribution at your current scope or prepare you for the next one.
