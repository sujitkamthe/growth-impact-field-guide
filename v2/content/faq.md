## Scopes

### Is this a checklist?

No. The lines on a scope page are signs of what impact looks like there, and none of them is a requirement on its own. At the team check, the question is whether we would trust you with a problem of this scope, not whether you did each item. [Self-assessment](#self-assessment/choosing-a-rating) says how each area is rated.

### What's the difference between Extended and Team?

At **Extended** you improve work beyond your own: a stream you lead, a design your teammates build on, a teammate you helped succeed. At **Team** you improve the system that produces the work: the architecture, the design bar, how people's judgment develops and how the team performs. The test: are you improving pieces of work, even other people's, or changing how the whole team produces its work?

### Does leading an account set my scope?

No. Many Sahaj accounts have a single team, so leading one doesn't by itself bring Team or Wider expectations; you are held to your own scope's expectations, whatever the role. The natural way to grow in it is to build the client relationship and explore how the account could grow, and that impact, which reaches past one team's delivery, is Wider.

### Do I need to work across several accounts, or speak at conferences?

No. Deep influence inside one client's organisation, or a capability many Sahaj teams use, counts as much as breadth across accounts. External work is one way to reach Wider scope, never a requirement for it.

### I'm new to Sahaj. Where do I start?

Your scope is agreed with your team from the impact you showed in your previous work, not from how long you have been at Sahaj, so you can start at any scope. For the first few weeks you'll be learning what's new to you, such as our stack, the client's domain or how we work, and nobody expects you to meet every expectation while you do. Begin on [the guide's front page](#home), which explains scopes and areas and helps you find the scope that matches your work, then read that scope's page.

## Growth

### Is delivering my work on time enough?

No. Finishing your work on time is part of Client & Delivery, not all of it. The area also covers building the work well, framing the client's problem with them, and owning commitments: questioning a date you don't believe in, keeping the client's expectations realistic and going back to them when something changes. Beyond that area, every scope expects impact in People & Team and Org & Community too. See [Client & Delivery](#area/client-delivery).

### How do I move to the next scope?

By keeping up your scope in all three areas, then taking on some of the next scope's work, or a greater contribution through your strength, until the people around you depend on you for it. Deepening your expertise at your current scope counts as growth too. See [Growing into the next scope](#how-it-works/growing-into-the-next-scope).

### Do I have to be equally strong in all three areas?

No. Your strength will vary by area, and each area's rating shows that. Strength in one area doesn't balance out falling well short in another, though: the area that fell short is below expectations, whatever your strengths elsewhere. You can still grow by going deep in what you do best; see [Specialisation](#specialisation).

### Can I take less of some work to focus on my strength?

Sometimes, from Extended scope, through an [agreed focus](#specialisation): you take less of a particular responsibility, a teammate who has room for it takes it on, and you contribute more through your strength. You can't step back from a whole area, and the standards stay with everyone.

### I'm already strong in every area. What next?

Talk with your team about a next challenge: a responsibility you haven't had before, a harder problem, or a stretch into the next scope. Exceeds is recorded whether or not your scope grows. If you often settle what falls between other people's work, that is impact of your own, so name it in your self-assessment, and don't take on every handover by default.

### Can I grow without leading a team?

Yes. At Team and Wider scope there are several ways to create impact, including platforms and standards, deep expertise and external work. Whichever way you take, you still need impact in People & Team and Org & Community.

## Contribution

### Is Org & Community really mandatory?

Yes. You choose how you contribute, usually through what you're good at, and every scope expects a contribution you keep up through the cycle, in working time. If your project leaves no room, see [How it is assessed](#area/org-community).

### Can I skip work I don't enjoy?

You can choose a different way to contribute, but not skip the area. Someone who doesn't enjoy writing can give a talk, interview or build a tool instead; agree the alternative with your team, so there's a contribution rather than a gap. Taking less of a whole responsibility, rather than skipping one activity, needs an [agreed focus](#specialisation).

### Can I contribute beyond my scope?

Yes. Your scope sets what's expected of you, and work beyond it counts for what it changed. Someone at Direct who shapes a design their teammates then build on is doing Extended work. Doing that consistently is a good reason to agree a stretch into the next scope.

### Does giving a conference talk make me more senior?

Not by itself. A talk counts for its depth and for what it led to, such as people changing how they work or opportunities it created.

### What if my project gives me no room to contribute or grow?

Agree it with the seniors on your team, or with your account lead if the cause is account-wide, as soon as you know; raised for the first time in your self-assessment, it's too late to change anything. [How it is assessed](#area/org-community) says what you agree and how you're rated.

## The process

### Who sets my expectations?

You do. At the start of the cycle you choose your scope, write your expectations doc and share it with your team. Anyone on the team can review it and ask for changes, whatever their scope. See [Who reviews and decides](#how-it-works/who-reviews-and-decides).

### What if the scope I choose doesn't match my impact?

The people reviewing it will say so, and the scope is corrected during the review, before your doc is agreed. That way your feedback and self-assessment are measured against the right scope. If you still disagree after discussing it, the team's seniors make a judgement call, and if that doesn't settle it, the account lead decides, or, for an account lead's own doc, a founder or the region's managing director.

### What if my expectations were never set?

That is the first thing to fix, before any self-assessment. Write your doc now, even mid-cycle, and get it reviewed.
